# THE INFLUENCE ENGINE — Fourteen Authors, One Toolkit

Brandon's named inspirations, converted into moves Aqualad can actually execute. These sharpen *his* voice (voice-bible) — they never replace it. Organized as: **what to steal · when to reach for it · how it lands in Brandon's work.**

Three entries are marked *(sharpen me)* — Aqualad knows these writers' disciplines but not which specific moves Brandon prizes. Per Tide Law 9, those entries stay lean until Brandon feeds them; upgrade them the session he does.

---

## Systems & structure

**BRANDON SANDERSON — the law-bound magic architect**
- *Steal:* Hard magic built on rules, costs, and limitations — the limitation is more interesting than the power. Promise → progress → payoff as the spine of every arc. The "avalanche" ending: converging payoffs detonating in sequence. Expand what exists before adding something new.
- *Reach for it when:* building or auditing any power system, plotting a novel, or an ending feels thin.
- *In Brandon's work:* Wesley's Influence already runs on Sanderson physics (cost, ledger, delayed collection). Every new system gets the three questions: what it costs, what it can't do, who knows the rules.

**WILLIAM D. ARAND — the systems-made-visible pragmatist**
- *Steal:* GameLit/LitRPG mechanics: progression the reader can track, transactional power economies, stakes expressed in resources. Pragmatic antihero POV that says the quiet strategy out loud. Relationship webs run with the same internal consistency as the magic.
- *Reach for it when:* a story wants visible progression (the Wolf contracts-and-credits economy, HALCYON build numbers), or an antihero's scheming needs to feel earned rather than convenient.
- *In Brandon's work:* Bianca's contract ledger and the watermark BUILD/DAY counters are already proto-systems — Arand's discipline keeps them consistent enough to bear plot weight.

## Myth & atmosphere

**NEIL GAIMAN — the myth in the room**
- *Steal:* Gods and folklore walking through the mundane world at full weight. Fairy-tale cadence in modern prose. The numinous treated matter-of-factly — dread through understatement, not volume. Stories that know they're stories.
- *Reach for it when:* the Ares/Olympus material, the angel/demon film, any scene where the supernatural enters an ordinary space and the temptation is to over-explain.
- *In Brandon's work:* The Night Court under the Jackson County Courthouse is a Gaiman door. The angel film's thesis — the divine reads as monstrous to the unready — is *American Gods* logic and deserves that restraint.

**ANDRZEJ SAPKOWSKI — the lesser-evil folklorist**
- *Steal:* Worldbuilding delivered almost entirely through dialogue and argument. Folklore inverted — the monster as mirror, the contract as morality play. "Lesser evil" choices with no clean exit. Banter that smuggles philosophy. Episodic short-story structure feeding a long arc.
- *Reach for it when:* Wolf takes a contract, any monster-of-the-week structure, or a moral dilemma is resolving too cleanly.
- *In Brandon's work:* Wolf is a Witcher-class protagonist — for-hire, grey, honest about it. Sapkowski's move: make the client worse than the monster, then make Wolf choose anyway.

**SIMON R. GREEN — the hidden-world noir showman**
- *Steal:* The secret world one wrong turn from ours, run on style and reputation. First-person swagger that narrates the weird like a tour guide with a gun. Escalation stacked in threes. Names as legends — everyone important carries a title and a rumor.
- *Reach for it when:* Trinity Noir, the Night Court, any first-person walk through a supernatural underworld, or a setting needs personality fast.
- *In Brandon's work:* Epithet culture (Demon of the Shadows, She-Wolf) is Green's economy of legend. Prima Facie's underworld courtrooms breathe Nightside air.

## Grounded genre

**PATRICIA BRIGGS — the working-class urban fantasist**
- *Steal:* A protagonist with a day job, bills, and a body that bruises. Pack/coven politics as social physics with rules the reader learns. Competence paired with real vulnerability. Romance as a slow structural thread, not a subplot bolted on.
- *Reach for it when:* a supernatural lead is floating free of ordinary life, or a romance thread needs to grow out of the plot instead of beside it.
- *In Brandon's work:* Wesley's coffee, joints, and unpaid caseload are the Briggs anchor that makes the Night Court land. Vivian & Desmond's slow-burn wants this pacing discipline.

**R.R. HAYWOOD — the propulsive ensemble runner**
- *Steal:* Serialized momentum — short chapters that sprint, each ending mid-stride. Found-family ensembles forged under catastrophe, every member earning their seat. Gallows humor as the pressure valve that makes horror survivable.
- *Reach for it when:* apocalypse/survival material (Dying Light, DayZ arcs), multi-character RP arcs, or a serial needs readers back tomorrow.
- *In Brandon's work:* The mission-arc RP structure (Arc One) is Haywood's engine; the banter-under-fire register is already native.

**DEAN KOONTZ — the dread metronome**
- *Steal:* Short chapters ending on turns — the micro-cliffhanger as pacing law. Ordinary decency versus articulate evil. Threat rendered in precise sensory detail. Dread built by rhythm: calm chapter, tightening chapter, detonation.
- *Reach for it when:* any thriller spine (CEO/Waiter, the angel film, Hunting White), or a manuscript reads well but doesn't *pull*.
- *In Brandon's work:* His chapter-end instinct is half-formed in the RP hooks (every post ends on a door). Koontz turns that courtesy into a publication-register law: no chapter ends at rest.

## Screen & sound

**RANDY McKINNON — the screen economist** *(sharpen me)*
- *Steal:* Screenwriter's discipline: character revealed through action, visual economy (what the camera sees is the sentence), grounded emotional truth inside genre frames, scene entry at the last possible moment.
- *Reach for it when:* CINEFLUX screen projects — beat sheets, the angel film, FACTORY SETTINGS video posts — and any prose scene that's all interiority and no behavior.
- *In Brandon's work:* Sunny's posts are 60-second screenplays; the slug-line habit shows he already thinks in frames. **Ask Brandon which McKinnon works/moves he prizes and upgrade this entry.**

**DIANDRA PENDLETON-THOMPSON — the sound-first dramatist** *(sharpen me)*
- *Steal:* Audio-native storytelling: scenes that establish space, character, and action through sound cues and dialogue alone; exposition carried by performance; the ear as the camera.
- *Reach for it when:* audio drama scripts (the Guardians format), FACTORY SETTINGS audio texture, any dialogue scene that would die on radio.
- *In Brandon's work:* The Guardians script already notates SFX beats and pronunciation guides — her discipline is the standard those scripts get held to. **Ask Brandon what he draws from her and upgrade this entry.**

**KARIN SLAUGHTER — the visceral crime surgeon** *(sharpen me)*
- *Steal:* Crime-thriller discipline where violence has weight and consequence — injuries that behave like injuries, procedure rendered exactly, dread built from forensic specificity rather than atmosphere. Female leads whose competence and damage coexist without either canceling the other. The unflinching scene that earns its brutality by never being decoration.
- *Reach for it when:* the Rabbit Hole's crime spine (the fire's paperwork, the records dig, the trap), any scene where violence or its aftermath is on the page, or a thriller thread is getting too clean.
- *In Brandon's work:* Jack's dislocation beat already runs on Slaughter physics — a wet mechanical pop, a man the color of paper, no movie crack. The amended discharge / 41-day fire file is procedural dread of exactly her species. **Ask Brandon which Slaughter works/moves he prizes and upgrade this entry.**

## Heat & the long game

**ALEATHA ROMIG — the dark-romance architect**
- *Steal:* Control and consent dynamics as the actual plot engine. Unreliable framing — scenes that reread differently after the reveal. Twists that recontextualize rather than merely surprise. Duet/trilogy structure where each book reframes the last. Heat that always does power-dynamic work.
- *Reach for it when:* the Alice–Rabbit Hole novel, the CEO/Waiter thriller, Evie, any erotic scene at risk of becoming filler.
- *In Brandon's work:* Talia-as-pursuer is a Romig inversion; the riddle-climax is heat doing plot work. Sunny's rewatch-sinister tics are the recontextualization move applied to horror.

**ALEXANDRA MONIR — the high-concept romantic**
- *Steal:* One clean speculative hook carrying a whole romance. Dual-timeline construction. Yearning as the narrative engine — desire sustained by obstacle, not delay for its own sake. Prose that stays clear at speed.
- *Reach for it when:* a romance premise needs its one-sentence hook sharpened, or a dual-timeline structure (the Alice novel's Alice-past/Talia-present potential) needs wiring.
- *In Brandon's work:* Every CINEFLUX romance already leads with a high-concept hook; Monir's test — can the hook fit in a logline and still ache? — is the SPAR-mode gate.

**MARGARET STOHL — the gothic franchise professional**
- *Steal:* Southern-gothic atmosphere — place as character, inheritance as curse. And the tie-in master's discipline: writing licensed characters with canon fidelity *plus* fresh interiority, finding the unexplored room in a house everyone knows.
- *Reach for it when:* any fandom RP work (the Marvel and game catalogs), collaborative/shared-universe writing, or an original setting needs gothic weight.
- *In Brandon's work:* His Quill sounds like Quill while being *his* Quill — that's the Stohl standard, now named. The SCP-style shared universe of FACTORY SETTINGS runs on the same franchise logic, self-owned.
