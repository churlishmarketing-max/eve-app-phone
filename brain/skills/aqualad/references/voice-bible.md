# THE VOICE BIBLE — Brandon's Style DNA

Extracted July 17, 2026 from the project archive (Prima Facie, the Wolf universe, Trinity Noir, JTR, Hunting White, the Ares Trials, the Evie files, the Guardians audio drama, In the End, and the bio/template catalog) plus the CINEFLUX project record. Every signature below carries a receipt from his own pages. This file is the law for DRAFT and DOCTOR modes.

## The two registers

Brandon writes for two worlds. Same voice, different rulebooks. **Establish register before touching a word.**

**RP REGISTER** — posts, replies, bios, prompt menus, comment threads for the roleplay community.
- Community conventions are *features*, not errors: "orbs" for eyes ("Her ocean like orbs turned to me"), color-coded dialogue per character, drop caps, epithets, dramatic taglines. Preserve them.
- Posts serve a partner: end on a hook or an open door the other writer can walk through.
- The coded HTML wrapper is part of the art (see formats.md).

**PUBLICATION REGISTER** — novels, screenplays, anything aimed at readers who paid.
- Sanderson/Gaiman-grade line standards. RP-isms convert: orbs → eyes; filter verbs (felt/saw/watched/heard) cut where the POV already implies them; said-bookisms thinned; "began to / started to" collapsed into the verb.
- Everything else in this file still applies — the voice survives the polish. The polish removes community shorthand, never personality.

When Brandon doesn't specify: RP characters and fandom work default to RP register; the novels (Alice–Rabbit Hole, Vivian & Desmond, Prima Facie-class work) and CINEFLUX screen projects default to publication register. Confirm once if genuinely unclear.

## Sixteen signatures (with receipts)

1. **The cold open + rewind.** Start at the crisis, then jump back. *"…laying on the front steps with my body riddled with bullet holes"* → **"Forty-Eight Hours Ago…"** (Trinity Noir). Nonlinear hooks are a house move — reach for them when a scene opens flat.

2. **Slug lines inside prose.** Location/time headers before scenes, screenplay-style even in narrative: *Universe 12Z6 / Trincity / Wolf's Office*. Keep them tight, three lines max.

3. **First-person noir wit.** The narrator undercuts dread with dry observation: *"The thermostat on the wall read seventy-two degrees. The thermostat was a liar."* / *"…made my bladder suggest that maybe I should have taken up accounting instead"* (Prima Facie). The joke never breaks tension — it banks it.

4. **Fragment beats.** Single-line paragraph punches for turns: *"Right. Wrong room for jokes."* / *"Damn it."* Deployed sparingly, they're his gear-shift. Overused, they're a tic — DOCTOR mode watches the ratio.

5. **Costed power systems.** Power always keeps a ledger: Wesley's Influence *"borrowing against a debt I can't see. The universe keeps a ledger, and it always collects."* New magic/powers get a price, a limit, and a collection date. Free power is off-brand and off-craft (see Sanderson, influence-engine).

6. **Numbers as texture.** Precision as characterization: the percentage sight (*"Kaelen accepts a straight deal: 12%"*), Bianca's tactical readouts (*"Fifty-one armed potential hostiles. One hundred seven civilians. Five exits, twelve possible exits."*). When a character is competent, show the math.

7. **Mythic bio openings.** Third-person legend cadence before the facts: *"It's said that those who aspire to greatness started at a young age… And in those eyes a monster was born"* (Wolf bio). Bios open like folklore, then earn the stat block.

8. **Weather as overture.** Openings and landings on atmosphere: *"Rain fell from the sky like God himself wept for the soul that had just returned home"* (JTR); Kansas City rain as relief after the Night Court. Weather carries the emotional key signature.

9. **Banter with bite.** Pop-culture deflection under pressure — Quill: *"No way Captain Crunch… We have a theme song, an action figure set, and a lunch box!"* The comedy character is never *only* comic; the jokes are armor and the reader can see the straps.

10. **The moral grey ledger.** Antiheroes state their own terms honestly: *"He was for himself, one thing he never lied about"* (Hunting White). Villains get arguments; heroes get costs. Corruption is the recurring enemy across universes.

11. **Epithets and legend-names.** Characters carry titles the world gave them: Demon of the Shadows, She-Wolf, The Ripper, God of War, Subject 01. New major characters should earn one — TOURNAMENT mode territory.

12. **The second voice.** A companion intelligence as counterpoint to the lead — Bianca (assistant, then ship AI), Kr'nch as Quill's foil, the watermark system as Sunny's silent commentator. The lead's voice sharpens against a second one; solo scenes are the exception to justify.

13. **Audience-aware design.** The Ares Trials menu: numbered prompts (001–010), each a situational pitch with direct/indirect involvement options, clear response instructions. Brandon designs *for the other writer's* fun. Every interactive deliverable keeps that courtesy.

14. **Genre subversion as architecture.** From the CINEFLUX record: the audience *is* the experiment (FACTORY SETTINGS); the pursuer is the woman (Talia); the monster is the protector (the angel film); the trope is the target (Desmond vs. the brooding-villain archetype). Find the genre's default assumption; invert it at the load-bearing level, not the paint level.

15. **Alliterative taglines.** *"SENSUAL, SEDUCTIVE, SINISTER."* / *"You Don't Know Jack."* Headers, bios, and coded posts carry a short blade of a tagline.

16. **Tics designed for rewatch.** Sunny's *"they know best"* — lines charming on first read, sinister on second. When a story has a reveal, seed dialogue that recontextualizes. (Romig's move — influence-engine.)

## Publication-register prose supplement (2026-07-22 — extracted from Brandon's own Rabbit Hole Ch1–Ch2 rewrites; his hand is the standard)

**The fingerprint, demonstrated:**
- **Lived-in interiority with self-aware humor mid-scene** — she jokes at herself while it's happening, not only in retrospect ("my closet is full of clothes for a girl who doesn't live in this body… some of them are really damn cute"; "a broke person's favorite currency: credit cards").
- **Mundane-specific grounding.** Silicone cups you "press on and pray over," TikTok-vs-movie memory framing, the drawer inventory ("something that buzzes, something that sucks, something that thrusts"). Pop-culture referents are in-voice (Batman villain, Michael Myers, The Strangers) — used by the narrator, never by the author.
- **Under-explain early, reveal late.** He moved the watch's dent OUT of Ch1 (kept vague: "something else") so it could debut in the card room. Specificity is a resource; spend it at the moment of maximum charge, not first mention.
- **Em-dash diet.** Vary the punctuation; a standalone "Fuck." is a legal sentence. Dialogue tags: comma + lowercase ("…happen," Mabel said) — never period-before-attribution.
- **Voice-to-text artifacts to fix silently** (growing list): determin→determine, hyperthermia→hypothermia, "talked it out of it"→"talked it out of", subject/verb garbles in long spoken sentences. Never restyle while fixing.

**The anti-AI kill list (DOCTOR mode enforces; these read as machine-made):**
- The "clerk in my head" / little-voice-narrator device. Dead. Replace with direct thought.
- Caption cadences: "that's the part/thing I keep," "here was the actual problem," "And here's the thing:" as paragraph engines. A human thinks the thought; she doesn't caption it.
- Audience asides ("that's how I tell it at brunch," "I want it on the record" as a tic) — first person is inner monologue, not testimony, unless the register is deliberately invoked ONCE as a named habit.
- Any refrain or gesture more than ~twice per chapter. Anaphora tricolons rationed to genuine high-tension beats.

**The heat register (for erotic work; see references/heat-playbook.md):**
- Sensual AND explicit, never vulgar: clit, wet, cum/cumming are in-voice; pussy/cunt are not. Anatomy is real; euphemism reads as cowardice, crudeness reads as a different book.
- The bar: reader's heart races, butterflies, the buzz — BookTok's "spice and story that made me finish it in 24 hours."
- Heat always does story work (Romig law): power, consent, control, and character move DURING the scene, not around it. Consent mechanics can be the eroticism (the complete-sentence rule).
- Bodies stay specific and human (adhesive sting, leather commentary, breath that won't line up) — never catalog, never choreography.

## Known tendencies DOCTOR mode manages

Not flaws — pressure points. Handle by register and never with mockery.

- **Voice-to-text homophones/mechanics** in drafts: *souls/soles, sewed/sowed, malace/malice, casted/cast, laxed/lax, much strong/much stronger, decenergrated/disintegrated.* Correct silently. Proper nouns: confirm canonical spelling once (his standing rule), then lock it in the canon ledger.
- **Filter-word density** in fast drafts (publication register only).
- **Fragment-beat frequency** — the punch dulls if thrown every paragraph.
- **Comma splices in banter** — often intentional rhythm in RP register; tighten only in publication register.
