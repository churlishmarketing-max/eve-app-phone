# Ad Formats

Every ad format Churlish Media produces, with structural templates, timing guidelines, and platform specifications.

## The Churlish call-out formula (core structure)

This is the DNA of every Churlish ad regardless of format. The sections compress or expand based on the format length, but the sequence never changes:

1. **Hook / pattern interrupt** (1–3 seconds video / first line static)
2. **The call-out** (pain identification with specific numbers and mirror language)
3. **The pivot** (reframe — blame the status quo, not the viewer)
4. **The proof** (specific results, names, timelines)
5. **The offer** (exactly what they get and what it costs)
6. **CTA** (one action, impossible to misunderstand)

For shorter formats (15s, static), sections 2–4 compress into a single beat. For longer formats (60s+), each section gets room to breathe.

## Video ad scripts

### 15-second script (Instagram/Facebook Reels, YouTube Shorts, TikTok)

**Total word count:** 35–45 words
**Structure:**
- Hook: 1 bold sentence (3 seconds)
- Pain + pivot: 1–2 sentences (5 seconds)
- Proof + offer: 1 sentence (4 seconds)
- CTA: 1 sentence (3 seconds)

**Format on page:**
```
[HOOK — ON SCREEN TEXT]
"Bold opening line."

[PAIN/PIVOT]
"Quick pain identification and reframe."

[PROOF + OFFER]
"Specific result. Here's what you get."

[CTA — ON SCREEN TEXT + VOICEOVER]
"Do this one thing now."
```

**Notes:** The hook must work with sound off (text overlay). Every sentence must be speakable in one breath. No filler words. Cut anything that doesn't move the viewer toward the CTA.

### 30-second script (Meta feed ads, YouTube pre-roll, CTV)

**Total word count:** 70–90 words
**Structure:**
- Hook: 1–2 sentences (3–5 seconds)
- Call-out: 2–3 sentences (8–10 seconds)
- Proof: 1–2 sentences (5–7 seconds)
- Offer + CTA: 2 sentences (8–10 seconds)

**Notes:** This is the workhorse format. Long enough to build tension, short enough to hold attention. The call-out section should include at least one specific number (dollar amount, percentage, timeframe).

### 60-second script (Meta, YouTube, CTV, long-form social)

**Total word count:** 140–170 words
**Structure:**
- Hook: 1–2 sentences (3–5 seconds)
- Call-out: 4–6 sentences (15–20 seconds) — this is where you go deep on the pain
- Pivot: 1–2 sentences (5–7 seconds)
- Proof: 2–3 sentences (10–12 seconds) — specific case study or result
- Offer: 2–3 sentences (8–10 seconds)
- CTA: 1–2 sentences (5 seconds)

**Notes:** The full call-out formula with room to breathe. The call-out section can use the "math breakdown" technique — walking the viewer through the actual numbers of their situation (margins, costs, revenue) until they feel the gap.

### 90-second to 2-minute script (YouTube mid-roll, long-form social, VSL-style)

**Total word count:** 200–300 words
**Structure:**
- Hook: pattern interrupt (5 seconds)
- Story open: personal or client story that sets up the problem (15–20 seconds)
- Call-out: expanded pain with data and industry context (20–30 seconds)
- Pivot: why the old way doesn't work (10–15 seconds)
- Proof: detailed case study with before/after and timeline (15–20 seconds)
- Offer: what they get, stacked value (10–15 seconds)
- CTA: urgency + one action (5–10 seconds)

**Notes:** Story-driven format. This is where the Russell Brunson "hook → story → offer" arc really kicks in. The story should be a real client transformation, not hypothetical.

## Static ad copy (Meta feed, Instagram, Facebook)

### Primary text (above the image/video)

**Character count:** 125 characters visible before "See more" on mobile. Full text can run 300–500 characters.

**Structure:**
- Line 1: Hook — must work in the 125-character preview. This is the scroll-stopper.
- Line 2–3: Pain call-out or provocative statement
- Line 4–5: Proof (one specific result)
- Line 6: Offer
- Line 7: CTA

**Formatting rules:**
- Use line breaks between sections (not a wall of text)
- No hashtags in the primary text (they look desperate)
- No emoji spam — one emoji max, and only if it adds meaning (a 🔥 after a strong number is fine; 🎉🚀💪 is not Churlish)
- If using a question as the hook, make it a question the reader can't scroll past without answering in their head

### Headline (below the image, next to the CTA button)

**Character count:** 40 characters max for full visibility
**Purpose:** Reinforce the offer or restate the hook. Not a place for new information.

**Examples:**
- "Your $5 sample is waiting."
- "47 leads in 21 days."
- "Stop competing on price."

### Description (below the headline — often hidden on mobile)

**Character count:** 30 characters max
**Purpose:** Supporting detail or urgency trigger
**Examples:**
- "Limited spots available"
- "Free strategy session"
- "Results in 90 days"

## Carousel ad copy (Meta)

### Structure: 3–5 cards, each with image + text

**Card 1 — The hook card:**
- Bold statement or provocative question
- Sets up the tension
- Visual: pattern interrupt image or bold text overlay on branded background

**Cards 2–3 — The pain/proof cards:**
- Each card addresses one specific pain point OR one proof point
- Keep text to 1–2 sentences per card
- Visual: data visualization, before/after, or client imagery with text overlay

**Card 4 (or 5) — The offer card:**
- Clear offer statement
- What they get + what it costs
- Visual: branded offer graphic with CTA

**Final card — CTA card:**
- One action
- Urgency if applicable
- Visual: clean branded graphic with the CTA button reinforced

**Carousel rules:**
- Each card must stand alone (someone swiping quickly should get the message from any single card)
- Each card must also pull the viewer to swipe to the next (create a narrative thread)
- Text on cards should be 15 words max — the image does the heavy lifting
- Primary text above the carousel follows the same rules as static ads

## UGC brief / creator script

When writing scripts for UGC creators (not Brandon on camera), the tone shifts slightly — it should sound like a real person sharing their experience, not like an ad. But the structure stays the same.

### UGC script structure:

**Hook (selfie-style, talking to camera):**
"Okay so I have to tell you about [product/service] because [unexpected claim or result]."

**The story (casual, personal):**
Walk through their experience — what the problem was, what they tried before, what changed. Use specific details that feel authentic (brand names of competitors, dollar amounts, timeframes).

**The result:**
One specific outcome stated plainly. No marketing language. "I saved $400 in the first month" not "I experienced significant cost optimization."

**The CTA:**
Soft sell. "If you're dealing with [the same problem], just try it" or "Link is in the bio, seriously just try the sample." The CTA should sound like advice from a friend, not a pitch.

### UGC brief format (what goes to the creator):

```
PRODUCT: [Name]
TARGET AUDIENCE: [Who this speaks to]
KEY MESSAGE: [The one thing they need to communicate]
TALKING POINTS: [3–4 bullet points of what to cover]
TONE: [Casual, authentic, slightly surprised by how well it worked]
DO: [Specific things to include — hold the product, show the result, mention the price]
DON'T: [Things to avoid — don't say "amazing," don't read from a script, don't use marketing jargon]
LENGTH: [15s / 30s / 60s]
FORMAT: [Vertical 9:16, selfie-style, natural lighting]
```

## CTV / Connected TV scripts (Vibe.co, programmatic)

### 15-second CTV spot

**Structure:** Tighter than a social 15s because there's no text overlay or "See more." Everything is in the voiceover and visual.

- Hook: 1 sentence (3 seconds)
- Value prop: 1 sentence (5 seconds)
- Proof: 1 beat — a number or a name (3 seconds)
- CTA: 1 sentence with URL or next step (4 seconds)

**Notes:** CTV viewers can't click. The CTA must be memorable — a short URL, a phone number, or "search for [brand name]." Keep it to one action that works from a couch.

### 30-second CTV spot

Same as the 30-second video script but with a CTV-appropriate CTA (URL on screen, phone number, or "ask Alexa to..." / "search [brand]").

## Platform-specific notes

### Meta (Facebook / Instagram)
- Vertical (9:16) for Reels and Stories
- Square (1:1) or 4:5 for feed
- Text overlays must work at mobile scale — 24pt minimum
- First 3 seconds determine whether someone watches or scrolls
- Sound-off optimization: captions/text overlays carry the message even on mute

### YouTube
- Horizontal (16:9) for standard, vertical (9:16) for Shorts
- Pre-roll ads: first 5 seconds are unskippable — the hook lives here
- Mid-roll: viewer is already engaged, can go deeper on story
- End screens and cards can supplement the CTA

### TikTok
- Vertical (9:16) only
- Native feel — should not look like an ad. UGC-style performs best.
- Trending audio can be used if relevant but never as a crutch
- Hook in first 1–2 seconds or you're dead

### LinkedIn
- Horizontal (16:9) or square (1:1)
- More professional tone but still Churlish — authoritative, not corporate
- Video autoplay is silent — text overlays are mandatory
- Longer primary text is acceptable (LinkedIn users read more)

### CTV (Vibe.co, programmatic)
- Horizontal (16:9) only
- No clickable CTAs — verbal/visual CTA only
- Geo-targeting is the primary targeting lever
- Frequency matters more than reach — repetition builds recognition

## Funnel stage guidance

### TOF (Top of funnel) — Awareness
- Goal: Stop the scroll. Introduce the problem. Create curiosity.
- Formats: 15s video, static with bold hook, carousel
- Tone: Provocative, challenging, pattern-interrupting
- CTA: Soft — "Learn more," "See how," or content consumption (watch the full video, read the post)

### MOF (Middle of funnel) — Consideration
- Goal: Build trust. Show proof. Handle objections.
- Formats: 30–60s video, case study carousel, UGC testimonial
- Tone: Authoritative, proof-heavy, empathetic
- CTA: Medium — "Book a call," "Get the free guide," "See the case study"

### BOF (Bottom of funnel) — Conversion
- Goal: Close. Overcome final hesitation. Make the offer irresistible.
- Formats: 15–30s retargeting video, offer-focused static, urgency-driven copy
- Tone: Direct, urgent (but not desperate), offer-focused
- CTA: Hard — "Buy now," "Claim your spot," "Start today." One action, one click.

## Full creative package structure

When the user asks for a "full ad package" or "creative package," produce all of the following for the campaign:

1. **3 TOF hooks** (15s video scripts or static primary text — designed for A/B testing)
2. **1 MOF proof ad** (30–60s video script with case study or detailed pain/solution)
3. **1 BOF offer ad** (15–30s with clear offer and urgency CTA)
4. **1 carousel** (4–5 cards following the hook → pain → proof → offer → CTA flow)
5. **Primary text variations** (2–3 versions of the above-the-fold copy for each ad)

Deliver as a .docx with sections clearly labeled by funnel stage and format.
