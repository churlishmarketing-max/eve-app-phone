# The Scoring Rubric

Score every call against this. Each section is scored 1–10 against the anchors below, then weighted.

## How the overall score is built

Overall (/100) = Σ (Section Score × Section Weight) ÷ 10. The weights are deliberately lopsided — discovery, objection handling, and the temp check carry the most because that's where deals are actually won and lost. The pitch is weighted low on purpose: presenting should be ~15% of the sale, not 50%. Points lost in the pitch are usually a symptom of a weak discovery, not a pitch problem.

## Score bands

- **90–100 Elite** — closer-level execution; replicate this call.
- **80–89 Strong** — closing-ready; fix 1–2 things and it closes consistently.
- **70–79 Competent** — the bones are there but leaving money on the table on execution.
- **55–69 Developing** — real structural gaps; deals slipping that shouldn't.
- **Below 55 Foundational** — rebuild the fundamentals; it's a selling problem, not a lead problem.

## Per-section anchors

### 1. Frame & Authority — weight 8
Did the rep set the doctor frame (diagnose before prescribing) and position as the expert, not the order-taker? Upfront agreement so nothing is a surprise?
- **9–10:** Clean upfront agreement, rep runs the call, expert energy without arrogance, never defends or claims "we're different."
- **7–8:** Frame mostly held; minor order-taking or over-explaining.
- **4–6:** Prospect is driving; rep answering instead of asking, or pitching/defending early.
- **1–3:** Pure order-taker; rambling, defensive, or led with credentials. No frame.

### 2. Discovery & Diagnosis — weight 16
Questions-only diagnosis. Opener ("what made you want to jump on a call?"), digging 3–4 layers deep, pulling baseline numbers (revenue, avg job size, leads/mo, close rate, ad spend, capacity).
- **9–10:** Almost all questions; peels every vague answer; gets the real numbers; prospect talks 70%+.
- **7–8:** Solid diagnosis, got numbers, but stopped a layer short on the emotional "why" a couple times.
- **4–6:** Surface-level; took first answers at face value; missing key numbers; started talking too soon.
- **1–3:** Barely diagnosed; jumped to the pitch with no picture of the business.

### 3. Pain — weight 9
Did the rep surface and let the prospect fully articulate the wound (biggest gaps, what they've tried, how long) before touching the solution?
- **9–10:** Prospect names their own pain in their own words and sits in it; rep stays quiet.
- **7–8:** Pain surfaced but rescued too fast.
- **4–6:** Pain mentioned, not developed; rep supplied it instead of extracting it.
- **1–3:** No real pain established; pitched into a vacuum.

### 4. Doubt — weight 7
Did the rep ask what's prevented them from solving it themselves / why past efforts failed? Surfaces the real objections early. Measurable and solvable — distinct from fear.
- **9–10:** Dug into why it isn't fixed and why past attempts failed, then used it to pre-frame the offer.
- **7–8:** Asked the doubt question but didn't fully mine past failures.
- **4–6:** Glossed over it; left hidden doubts to surface later as objections.
- **1–3:** Never asked; got ambushed by objections that should have surfaced here.

### 5. Cost / Consequence — weight 9
Did the rep quantify what the problem costs (dollars + emotional toll) and what happens if nothing changes? Makes the price feel small.
- **9–10:** Made the prospect calculate the cost of inaction and feel it; used current-vs-desired-state.
- **7–8:** Touched the cost but didn't fully monetize or let it sting.
- **4–6:** Vague "this is costing you" with no numbers.
- **1–3:** Skipped — which is why the price felt big later.

### 6. Desire & Future-Pacing — weight 8
Did the rep excavate what the prospect actually wants — granular goals, the real number they need, must vs. nice-to-have — so the investment anchors to something real?
- **9–10:** Specific goals in their own words, a monthly number tied to them, commitment level confirmed.
- **7–8:** Got the goals but left them abstract, or didn't pin the number.
- **4–6:** Surface goals only ("make more money").
- **1–3:** Never went here; money has nothing to attach to.

### 7. Temp Check / Lock-Before-Price (1–10) — weight 10
Did the rep take the temperature ("on a scale of 1–10… you can't say 7" / "how confident are you this is what you need?") and get the prospect to voice their own confidence BEFORE any price? Refuse to advance while confidence is low.
- **9–10:** Ran the check, got the number + "why" out loud, only moved to price at a genuine 9–10; if lower, stopped and resolved the doubt first.
- **7–8:** Took the temperature but advanced on a soft 8.
- **4–6:** Weak/rushed check, or moved to price with obvious hesitancy on the table.
- **1–3:** No temp check; revealed price into uncertainty.

### 8. Pitch (present by not presenting) — weight 7
Short, framed as the machine (not tasks), pointed only at the problems the prospect named. Permission first; checking-for-agreement questions. Weighted low on purpose — more pitch is not better.
- **9–10:** Asked permission, kept it tight, sold the outcome/machine, mapped to stated problems, checked agreement.
- **7–8:** Good pitch, slightly long or drifted into an unneeded feature.
- **4–6:** Feature-dump; pitched the whole solution instead of their problems.
- **1–3:** Long monologue, led with features, no agreement checks.

### 9. Price Reveal & Silence — weight 5
State the number cleanly and calmly, then stop talking and let the prospect speak first. Lead with the clean structure; only surface the payment plan if money is truly the blocker.
- **9–10:** Stated it flat, no flinch, no over-explaining, then silence; prospect spoke first; only neutral re-entry ("what are your thoughts?").
- **7–8:** Clean reveal but stepped on the silence or over-explained slightly.
- **4–6:** Rushed/apologetic delivery, filled the silence, or led with the payment plan / discount unprompted.
- **1–3:** Fumbled the number, talked through the reaction, or negotiated against themselves first.

### 10. Objection Handling — weight 15
The full loop — **Isolate → reframe/stack the belief → tie down the new belief (get the yes) → re-ask for the sale.** Money-vs-fear delineation. Open wallet on financial objections. Never arguing. Doubt (resolve it) vs. fear (normalize it).
- **9–10:** Isolated granularly ("assuming that's handled, is there anything else stopping you right now?"), reframed, tied down, re-asked immediately; ran open wallet on money; correctly split fear from doubt; never argued.
- **7–8:** Handled it but skipped the isolate or forgot to re-ask after the tie-down.
- **4–6:** Took the objection at face value; solved before isolating; folded to monthly on first money resistance without open wallet.
- **1–3:** Argued, got defensive, or accepted "I need to think about it" / "no money" and ended the call.

### 11. Close & Next Steps — weight 6
Lock the commitment, handle payment cleanly (ideally keep them on screen through checkout), book the kickoff, pre-empt buyer's remorse. If it didn't close, set up the follow-up.
- **9–10:** Assumptive next steps, kept control through the transaction, booked kickoff, reinforced the decision; if not closed, locked a real next step + response mechanism.
- **7–8:** Closed cleanly but loose on kickoff booking or remorse prevention.
- **4–6:** Got the yes but left logistics vague.
- **1–3:** No clear next step, no follow-up plan.
