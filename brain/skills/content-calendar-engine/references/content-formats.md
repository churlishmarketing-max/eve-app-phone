# Content Formats

Every content format Churlish Media uses in calendars, with structure, specs, and funnel-stage alignment.

## Feed post types

### 1. Results post (Client win)

**Funnel stage:** MOF or BOF
**Purpose:** Prove the system works with specific, verifiable results

**Structure:**
- Visual: Client imagery with cinematic grade (teal shadows, crushed blacks) or bold result number as text overlay
- Hook: The specific result stated plainly ("47 leads in 21 days.")
- Body: 2–3 sentences of context — who the client is, what the situation was, what changed
- CTA: Depends on funnel stage — MOF: "Want to see how?" / BOF: "Book your strategy call"

**Caption framework:**
```
[RESULT — bold, specific]
[1 sentence: who they are and where they started]
[1–2 sentences: what we built for them]
[1 sentence: the outcome]
[CTA]
```

### 2. Behind the lens (Process / BTS)

**Funnel stage:** MOF
**Purpose:** Build trust by showing the work behind the results. Humanize the brand.

**Structure:**
- Visual: Production shots, setup photos, candid moments from shoot days — same cinematic treatment as everything else
- Hook: Something specific and interesting about the process ("Every frame is intentional." / "This is what a $3,500/month content system looks like on shoot day.")
- Body: What's happening, why it matters, what the client gets from this moment
- CTA: Soft — engagement-focused ("What's behind the scenes of YOUR business?")

### 3. Sharp take (Contrarian POV)

**Funnel stage:** TOF
**Purpose:** Challenge conventional wisdom to drive engagement and establish authority

**Structure:**
- Visual: Bold text on dark background, or video of Brandon delivering the take to camera
- Hook: The contrarian statement itself ("Your marketing agency is the most expensive employee you'll never fire.")
- Body: Expand on why the conventional wisdom is wrong. Use the uncomfortable truth formula. Back it with logic or data.
- CTA: Engagement prompt — "Agree or disagree?" / "Tell me I'm wrong."

**Caption framework:**
```
[CONTRARIAN HOOK — bold, provocative]

[2–3 sentences expanding the argument]
[1 sentence of proof or logical consequence]

[Engagement prompt — NOT a sales CTA]
```

### 4. The proof (Testimonial / social proof)

**Funnel stage:** BOF
**Purpose:** Let someone else sell for you. Third-party validation is more persuasive than anything the brand can say about itself.

**Structure:**
- Visual: Client video testimonial clip, screenshot of a text/email/review, or quote graphic
- Hook: The most powerful line from the testimonial, pulled as a quote
- Body: Brief context on who the client is and what they were dealing with before. Let the testimonial do the heavy lifting.
- CTA: Direct — "Ready for your own story like this? [Link/DM/CTA]"

### 5. Educational / value post

**Funnel stage:** TOF
**Purpose:** Teach something useful. Demonstrate expertise. Give value with no ask.

**Structure:**
- Visual: Carousel (most common), video explainer, or infographic
- Hook: Frame the problem or question the content answers ("Most businesses waste 80% of their content. Here's the 20% that actually converts.")
- Body: The actual value — tips, framework, breakdown, or tutorial
- CTA: Soft — "Save this for later" / "Share with someone who needs this"

**Carousel structure for educational posts:**
- Card 1: Hook (bold statement or question)
- Cards 2–4: The value (one point per card, concise)
- Card 5: Summary + soft CTA

### 6. Personal / brand story

**Funnel stage:** MOF
**Purpose:** Build connection and relatability. People buy from people they feel like they know.

**Structure:**
- Visual: Personal photo, candid moment, or video diary-style clip
- Hook: A moment, a realization, or a vulnerable statement ("I almost quit three years ago.")
- Body: The story — keep it real, keep it tight, and connect it to a lesson that's relevant to the audience
- CTA: Engagement — "Has anyone else been here?" / share a related experience

### 7. Offer / promo post

**Funnel stage:** BOF
**Purpose:** Present a specific offer with urgency

**Structure:**
- Visual: Offer graphic with clear pricing/value prop, or video with offer breakdown
- Hook: The offer stated clearly ("$5 sample. No commitment. See for yourself.")
- Body: What they get, why it's valuable, what makes it urgent or limited
- CTA: Direct, singular — one action, one link, one button

**Rules for offer posts:**
- No more than 1 offer post per week (oversaturation kills trust)
- Always preceded by value and proof content earlier in the week
- The offer should feel like a natural next step, not a sudden sales pitch

## Video format specs

### Short-form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok)
- **Aspect ratio:** 9:16 (vertical)
- **Resolution:** 1080x1920 minimum
- **Length:** 15–60 seconds (sweet spot: 30–45s)
- **Captions:** Burned in (open captions) — never rely on platform auto-captions
- **Caption style:** Sans-serif, accessible size (minimum 24pt equivalent), high contrast against background
- **Safe zones:** Keep text within the center 80% to avoid being covered by UI elements

### Long-form (YouTube)
- **Aspect ratio:** 16:9 (horizontal)
- **Resolution:** 1920x1080 minimum (4K preferred)
- **Length:** 8–15 minutes (sweet spot for authority content)
- **Chapters:** Add chapter markers for any video over 5 minutes
- **Thumbnail:** Custom — bold text, expressive face, high contrast, teal/red accent

### Square (Feed-optimized)
- **Aspect ratio:** 1:1
- **Resolution:** 1080x1080
- **Use case:** Facebook feed, LinkedIn, some Instagram placements

### Portrait (Feed-optimized)
- **Aspect ratio:** 4:5
- **Resolution:** 1080x1350
- **Use case:** Instagram feed (takes up more screen real estate than 1:1)

## Carousel specs

- **Aspect ratio:** 1:1 or 4:5 (4:5 recommended for Instagram — more screen space)
- **Card count:** 4–10 cards (sweet spot: 5–7)
- **Font size on cards:** Minimum 24pt for body, 32pt+ for headlines
- **Text per card:** 15–25 words max — cards are visual, not articles
- **First card:** Must function as a hook even without context (it's what appears in the feed)
- **Last card:** CTA + profile tag for reshares

## Caption writing rules

These apply to all social platforms. Always follow the voice guard tone.

1. **Hook in the first line.** The preview on most platforms shows ~125 characters. The hook must live in that space.
2. **Line breaks between sections.** Walls of text get skipped. Use whitespace.
3. **One CTA per caption.** Not "like, comment, share, and check the link in bio." One action.
4. **No hashtag spam.** 3–5 relevant hashtags maximum on Instagram, placed at the end or in a comment. Zero hashtags on Facebook and LinkedIn (they hurt reach).
5. **No emoji as punctuation.** One emoji is fine if it adds meaning. A row of emojis is never Churlish.
6. **Write for the ear.** Read the caption out loud. If it sounds stiff, conversational, or like it was generated by a corporate marketing team, rewrite it.

## Platform-specific caption notes

### Instagram
- Hook must work in 125-character preview
- Can go longer (up to 2,200 characters) — people do read long captions here if the hook earns it
- Hashtags: 3–5, placed at the end or in the first comment
- Tag collaborators in the image, not the caption

### Facebook
- Shorter captions perform better (under 80 words for most post types)
- No hashtags
- Link posts: put the URL in the comments, not the caption (avoids reach suppression)
- Facebook rewards native video — always upload directly, never share a YouTube link

### LinkedIn
- Text-first posts perform extremely well — you don't always need an image
- Longer-form storytelling works here (300–600 words)
- No hashtags (or 1–2 max, industry-specific)
- First line must hook — LinkedIn shows ~140 characters before "see more"

### YouTube (descriptions)
- First 2 lines appear in search results and above the fold — put the hook and key info here
- Include timestamps/chapters for videos over 5 minutes
- 3–5 relevant tags in the description
- Include a CTA with link (subscribe, book a call, visit the site)

### TikTok
- Ultra-short captions (under 50 words)
- The video IS the content — caption is supporting, not primary
- Trending hashtags can help discoverability but don't force them
- CTA in the video itself, not just the caption
