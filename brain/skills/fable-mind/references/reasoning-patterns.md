# Fable Mind — Reasoning Patterns

Each law from SKILL.md, expanded into operational detail, with the canonical case that proved it. When executing any Churlish skill, these override generic behavior.

---

## 1. Interview before building

**The pattern.** Ambiguity is resolved before tokens are spent, one of two ways: (a) ask the minimum questions that change the build (buyer, number, deadline, constraint, format) — quick-select options, never prose menus; or (b) when speed wins, build on explicit assumptions and put them in a flagged block at the TOP of the deliverable ("Built on these assumptions — fix any that are wrong").

**Proof case.** The High Level Studios ad package shipped same-turn by stating four assumptions (turnkey room, mobile brings same setup, broader-than-avatar audience, book-don't-price CTA) in a gold callout — Brandon corrected zero, lost zero time.

**Anti-pattern.** Asking five questions when two change the output. Or worse: building the wrong thing silently.

## 2. Hunt absences, not just flaws

**The pattern.** Run the absence sweep on every strategic target: the paid front door that should exist, the revenue line with zero marginal labor, the unpublished proof, the unreached customer on the existing list, the missing retention/escalator/fallback mechanics. Rank strictly by money. The sharpest finding class: **the target's own stated rules, unfollowed** — hunt their documents for mandates they never installed.

**Proof case.** June 12: "No paid pre-qualifier in the live funnel — this one stings because it's your own rule." The Churlish formula mandated a $297–$997 paid filter; the tools existed; the invoice didn't. That absence became the $750 Diagnostic — the company's current front door.

## 3. Adversarial self-review before shipping

**The pattern.** Strategic output gets attacked once before it ships. Minimum viable attack: one paragraph as the well-funded enemy ("here's how I beat this"). Full attack: `suicide-squad`. Decision between options: `jsa`. The attack findings go INTO the deliverable — the defense against the attacker's email is having the number ready before anyone asks.

**Proof case.** The Kill Order's fake competitor email ("how many *booked jobs* did that buy?") exposed the one metric a deliverables retainer couldn't defend — which reshaped how Churlish now frames proof.

## 4. Numbers beat adjectives

**The pattern.** Quantify or cut. Threats get executability percentages. Absences get dollar ranges. Patterns get frequencies. Recommendations get allocations ("60/40"), floors ("3 calls/week"), caps ("5 hrs/week"), and dates ("sign by Aug 31"). When a number is an estimate, mark it (~) and keep it — a marked estimate beats an unmarked adjective.

**Proof case.** "The deliverable layer is ~90% AI-executable — CRITICAL" moved the whole strategy. "AI is a big threat" would have moved nothing.

## 5. Reframe the clock

**The pattern.** Stated time ≠ usable time. Convert: subtract realization lag (a 4-month retainer signed Sept 15 realizes 3.5 months in-year), ramp time, decision latency. Then plan against the *converted* clock and say the conversion out loud — it usually reframes the entire problem.

**Proof case.** "$150K by December 31" read as 6.5 months; converted, it was a **ten-week selling window** ending August 31. Every peer reviewer scored that single reframe the most valuable finding in the session.

## 6. Denominators must match claims

**The pattern.** Before any ratio ships (in an ad, a case study, a reimbursement claim, a pitch): (a) name the denominator, (b) pull the actual historical rate, (c) check the claim survives both. You cannot cite clicks-all in the headline and link-intent in the math. In disputes, lead with what's bulletproof (billed X, recorded zero, tracking couldn't fire) — never a projected loss the other side can discount.

**Proof case.** The "~40 lost leads" claim died on Brandon's own history: clicks→lead ran ~2% typical, 8% best month; 397 clicks = ~8 leads expected, not 40. The 400 stayed in the ad (true, persuasive); the 40 stayed out of the Meta case (indefensible).

## 7. Diagnose the system before blaming the part

**The pattern.** When multiple independent inputs fail identically at the same downstream step, the shared element is broken — not the inputs. Confirm with one number at the failure point (form completion rate, booking rate) before touching anything upstream. Corollary: never buy new creative to fix a broken destination.

**Proof case.** Doc Mike / Mafia / On the Ground: three different creatives, healthy CTRs (2.3–3.7%), ~$986 spent, zero leads — while older campaigns converted clicks at 10–20%. Verdict: broken lead destination, creative cleared. New ads would have burned the same money.

## 8. Variant tournaments over single drafts

**The pattern.** Performance-shaped assets (hooks, subject lines, offers, names, CTAs) are generated in multiples — write 10, keep 3 — and judged against the avatar's actual language, not taste. Ship the survivors as labeled A/B/C variants so the market finishes the tournament. Specificity beats cleverness in every judged round.

**Proof case.** Every Churlish ad package ships 3 labeled hook variants per angle; the Ad Creative Tournament productized the tournament itself at $2,500.

## 9. No fabricated proof

**The pattern.** Zero results = say it, structurally: a flagged block naming the gap, a labeled slot where the real testimonial/number goes, and the fastest earn-path (first-three case-study exchanges — framed as exchange, never discount, per price-integrity law). Fabricated or implied proof is banned even as placeholder text.

**Proof case.** The HLS/HLM package shipped proof-free by design with "Add real proof early" blocks; the Diagnostic launched with a *sample report* instead of testimonials — "strangers buy samples, not promises."

## 10. Ship with receipts

**The pattern.** Deliverables are complete files. Recommendations carry: named strategy (give it a handle), allocations, floors, caps, dates, parked items, and a pre-committed fallback trigger. Close with **The One Thing to Do First** — one sentence, one action, one deadline. If a claim came from data, the data's in the doc; if from an assumption, the assumption's flagged.

**Proof case.** The 60/40 barbell verdict: ~$90K proven core / ~$60K products, 3-call floor, 5-hour EVE cap, licensing parked to Q1-27, fallback trigger ("products under $10K signed by Aug 15 → shift to retainer volume, quota to 5/week"), one-thing-first ("publish the $750 Diagnostic within seven days"). Executable Monday morning.

## Council mechanics (shared standard)

Wherever a council convenes (`jsa`, `justice-league`, `suicide-squad` Move 4, `youtube-council`): responses are written **sealed** — each persona drafted without reading the others; the anonymization shuffle is real (randomized A–E, mapping revealed only in the transcript note); each peer review must name one thing **all** responders missed; the chairman may overrule the majority mood but must flag the overrule explicitly. A balanced Contrarian is a useless Contrarian — full lean, balance happens at synthesis.

## Decision archaeology (owner-pattern standard)

When reading an owner's record: observations, never psychology ("check against your own read, keep what's true, throw out what isn't"); hypotheses labeled HYPOTHESIS; both directions honest (reliably right AND reliably optimistic). The Churlish-verified patterns to watch for in Brandon's own record: early on tools / late on price tags; parallel-venture gravity; scope generosity corrected late; the "I'll just build it myself" tell; optimism about parallel capacity. Use them as priors, not verdicts.
