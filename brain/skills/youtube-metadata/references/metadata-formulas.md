# Metadata Formulas

## Title formulas

YouTube titles need to do two things simultaneously: rank in search (contain the keyword) and earn the click (create curiosity or promise value). These formulas balance both.

### Formula 1: The specific result
**Structure:** "[Specific outcome] — [How / context]"
**Examples:**
- "47 Leads in 21 Days — The Exact System We Used"
- "$60,000 Saved — Why Smart Woodworkers Are Switching Finishes"
- "From 0 to 40 Clients in 6 Months — What Nobody Tells You"

**When to use:** Case study videos, results-driven content. The specific number does the heavy lifting.

### Formula 2: The "How to" with a twist
**Structure:** "How to [Desired outcome] (Without [Common fear/objection])"
**Examples:**
- "How to Get Roofing Leads (Without Cold Calling)"
- "How to Build a Furniture Business (Without Selling Your Soul on Etsy)"
- "How to Create Content That Actually Gets Leads"

**When to use:** Educational content targeting people actively searching for solutions.

### Formula 3: The contrarian / challenge
**Structure:** "[Thing everyone believes] Is [Wrong/Killing your business]"
**Examples:**
- "Your Marketing Agency Is Lying to You"
- "Consistency Is NOT Why Your Content Fails"
- "Stop Posting Every Day — It's Hurting Your Brand"

**When to use:** Hot take videos, opinion pieces, myth-busting content. Drives high CTR from curiosity.

### Formula 4: The insider / behind-the-scenes
**Structure:** "[What we/I did] for [Client/Project] — [Teaser of outcome]"
**Examples:**
- "We Built a $500K Marketing System for a Local Business"
- "How I'd Market a Roofing Company in 2026"
- "Inside Our $3,500/Month Content Machine"

**When to use:** Process videos, client project breakdowns, authority positioning.

### Formula 5: The list / framework
**Structure:** "[Number] [Things] Every [Avatar] Needs to [Outcome]"
**Examples:**
- "5 Videos Every Contractor Needs on Their Website"
- "The 3-Part System That Turns Content Into Clients"
- "7 Mistakes Killing Your Marketing (And What to Do Instead)"

**When to use:** Framework videos, educational series, evergreen content.

### Formula 6: The question
**Structure:** "[Question the avatar is already asking themselves]?"
**Examples:**
- "Why Isn't Your Content Getting Leads?"
- "Is Your Business Invisible to 90% of Your Customers?"
- "What Happens When You Stop Competing on Price?"

**When to use:** TOF awareness content. The question should match a real search query when possible.

### Title rules:
1. **Under 60 characters.** YouTube truncates at ~60 characters in search results. Everything after that is invisible.
2. **Primary keyword in the first half.** YouTube's algorithm weights the beginning of the title.
3. **No ALL CAPS titles.** One or two capitalized words for emphasis is fine ("This Changed EVERYTHING"). Full caps titles look spammy.
4. **No clickbait without payoff.** The video must deliver what the title promises. Churlish doesn't bait-and-switch.
5. **Write 5, pick 3.** Always generate more options than needed so the user can choose based on their priority.

## Description template

```
[HOOK — 2 lines that appear above the fold in search results. Must contain the primary keyword naturally and create enough curiosity to click "Show more." This is the most important part of the description.]

[PARAGRAPH — 3-5 sentences summarizing what the video covers, who it's for, and what they'll learn or see. Integrate 2–3 secondary keywords naturally. Do not keyword-stuff.]

⏱️ CHAPTERS
[00:00] — [Hook-style chapter title]
[02:15] — [Hook-style chapter title]
[05:30] — [Hook-style chapter title]
[etc.]

🔗 LINKS
[Relevant links — client website, tools mentioned, lead magnet, booking page]

📲 CONNECT
[Channel/brand social links]
[Brandon's social links if relevant]

🔔 SUBSCRIBE for [what the channel delivers — e.g., "weekly strategies for service-based businesses"]

#[PrimaryKeyword] #[SecondaryKeyword] #[BrandTag]
```

### Description rules:
1. **First 2 lines are everything.** They show in search results and above the fold. Treat them like ad copy — they must earn the click to "Show more."
2. **Natural keyword integration.** Include the primary keyword in the first 25 words. Include 2–3 secondary keywords in the body. Never force them.
3. **Chapters are mandatory for 5+ minute videos.** They improve watch time (people jump to relevant sections) and appear in search results as visual previews.
4. **One CTA in the description.** Not five links to everything you've ever made. One clear next step.
5. **Hashtags at the end, 3 max.** YouTube shows the first 3 hashtags above the title. Make them count.

## Tag strategy

Tags are less important than they used to be for YouTube SEO, but they still help with discoverability in suggested videos and help YouTube understand what your video is about.

### Tag structure (15–25 tags):

**Primary keyword (1–2 tags):**
- Exact match of the main search term
- Slight variation (singular/plural, reordered)

**Secondary keywords (3–5 tags):**
- Related search terms that describe the video's content
- Questions people search related to the topic

**Broad topic tags (3–5 tags):**
- The industry or category (e.g., "roofing marketing," "woodworking business")
- The content type (e.g., "marketing strategy," "business tips")

**Long-tail keywords (5–8 tags):**
- Specific phrases with lower search volume but higher relevance
- These are where you win against bigger channels
- Example: "how to market a roofing company in omaha" vs. just "roofing marketing"

**Channel/brand tags (2–3 tags):**
- Channel name
- Brand name
- Series name (if applicable)

### Tag rules:
1. Primary keyword is ALWAYS the first tag
2. No irrelevant tags — YouTube penalizes misleading tags
3. No single-word tags (too broad, too competitive)
4. Mix exact-match and natural-language variations

## Thumbnail principles

### Composition rules:
- **3–5 words max as text overlay.** If you need more words, the concept isn't clear enough.
- **Face + emotion.** Thumbnails with an expressive human face consistently outperform text-only or graphic-only thumbnails.
- **High contrast.** The thumbnail must read at the size of a postage stamp — that's how most people see it (mobile search results, suggested sidebar).
- **One focal point.** The viewer's eye should go to one place. Multiple competing elements reduce click-through.

### Churlish thumbnail style:
- Bold sans-serif text (Barlow Condensed or similar heavy font)
- Dark background or desaturated image with one bright accent
- Teal and/or red accent (consistent with brand — but red is punctuation, not the whole design)
- The client or Brandon's face with a strong expression (surprise, intensity, skepticism — not a generic smile)
- No stock photo aesthetic — must feel real and cinematic

### Thumbnail text formulas:
- **The number:** "47 LEADS" / "$60K SAVED" / "3 MISTAKES"
- **The question:** "WHY NO LEADS?" / "IS THIS WRONG?"
- **The challenge:** "STOP THIS NOW" / "YOU'RE LOSING MONEY"
- **The reveal:** "THE REAL COST" / "WHAT THEY DON'T SAY"

### What NOT to do:
- No arrows pointing at nothing
- No red circles around random objects
- No shocked face for content that isn't shocking
- No more than 2 colors in the text
- No thumbnails that misrepresent the video content

## Chapter marker style

Chapters should use hook-style titles, not generic labels. The viewer scanning chapters should feel curious about each section.

**Instead of:**
- 00:00 — Introduction
- 02:30 — The Problem
- 05:00 — The Solution
- 08:00 — Results

**Write:**
- 00:00 — Why 90% of contractors are invisible online
- 02:30 — The real reason your ads aren't working
- 05:00 — The "one shoot, five channels" system
- 08:00 — 47 leads in 21 days (the actual numbers)

Each chapter title should work as a mini-hook that makes someone want to watch that section.
