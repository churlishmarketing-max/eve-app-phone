# Proposal Architecture

Every Churlish Media proposal follows this structure. The sections are ordered deliberately — each one builds on the last to move the prospect from "I have a problem" to "this is the solution" to "here's what it costs" to "here's what to do next."

## Section 1: Cover Page

- Prospect business name (large, prominent)
- "A Strategic Proposal by Churlish Media"
- Date
- Prepared for: [Contact Name]
- Churlish Media logo area (or text-based branding: "CHURLISH MEDIA" in Arial Black)
- Accent: single teal divider line

Keep it clean. No paragraphs on the cover. The cover's job is to feel premium and specific to them.

## Section 2: The Situation (Diagnosis)

This is the most important section. It proves you understand their business before you've pitched anything. It should read like a diagnosis — "here's what I see when I look at your business."

Structure:
1. Open with a direct observation about their current position (use data from their website, social, or discovery call)
2. Name the gap between where they are and where they could be
3. Identify the root cause (not symptoms) — usually one of: no authority positioning, no content system, competing on price instead of trust, inconsistent presence, no lead capture infrastructure
4. Frame the cost of staying where they are (opportunity cost, not scare tactics)

**Tone:** You're the doctor explaining the X-ray, not the salesman explaining the brochure. Confident, specific, empathetic but honest.

**Length:** 3–5 paragraphs. No more. This section should be tight.

**Example opening lines (adapt to prospect):**
- "Right now, [Business Name] is one of [N] [industry] companies in the [City] metro. You're good at what you do — but from the outside looking in, there's no way for a potential customer to know that."
- "[Contact Name], your work speaks for itself — to anyone who's already hired you. The problem is that the people who haven't hired you yet can't hear it."
- "You've built a business that does [$X] a year on reputation alone. Imagine what happens when you add a system that compounds that reputation 24/7."

## Section 3: The Approach (Strategy Overview)

Name the methodology: The Authority Engine. Briefly explain what it is and why it works for their specific situation.

Structure:
1. One paragraph explaining the Authority Engine concept: positioning the business owner as the recognized expert in their market through story-driven video, strategic content, and direct-response advertising
2. 3–4 bullet points on what this looks like for THEIR business specifically (not generic — reference their industry, their competitors, their market)
3. The "one shoot, multiple channels" leverage concept — one video shoot becomes YouTube content, social clips, ad creative, website assets, and photos

**Do not list all services here.** That's the next section. This section sells the philosophy and the "why this approach" — not the "what you get."

## Section 4: The Recommendation (Tiered Scope)

Present the tiers side by side. Always two or three tiers. The structure creates contrast — the lower tier shows the prospect what they'd be missing, the higher tier shows what full commitment looks like, and the middle tier (if three) becomes the obvious choice.

### Two-tier format:
- **Tier 1 (entry):** The essential engine — content + ads + basic lead gen. Lower price, shorter commitment. Prospect handles their own CRM and follow-up.
- **Tier 2 (full):** The complete system — content + ads + CRM + automations + lead tracking. Higher price, longer commitment. Churlish handles everything.

### Three-tier format:
- **Tier 1 (Launchpad):** Entry point — Authority Launchpad ($2,500 one-time). One owned build, no retainer. Swap in Organic Authority Engine ($3,000/mo, 4-month minimum) instead if they want an ongoing organic channel with no ads.
- **Tier 2 (Engine):** The sweet spot — Authority Engine ($3,500/mo, 4-month minimum). Content + ads. Everything most businesses need.
- **Tier 3 (System):** Full infrastructure — Authority System ($5,000/mo, 6-month minimum). Adds full CRM + automations + lead dashboard. For businesses ready to scale aggressively.

### How to present each tier:

Use a comparison table with rows for each deliverable category. Green checkmarks or specific quantities for included items. Gray dashes or "You handle this" for excluded items.

Categories to include in every tier comparison:
- Video shoots per month
- Short-form content pieces
- Brand photography (dedicated session vs. frame grabs)
- Edited photos (quantity range)
- Ad management (included/not, ad spend noted as separate)
- CRM + automations
- Lead tracking dashboard
- Strategy calls (frequency)
- Commitment term

Below the table, write 1–2 sentences per tier explaining who it's for — not what's in it (the table already shows that), but what type of business owner should choose it.

## Section 5: Investment

State the monthly price for each tier clearly. Include:
- Monthly investment amount
- Commitment term (Authority Engine and Organic Authority Engine: 4-month minimum; Authority System: 6-month minimum; Authority Launchpad: a one-time build, no term)
- Ad spend note — **ads-managed tiers only (Authority Engine, Authority System):** "Ad spend is separate — $500/month minimum recommended." **Never write this line for Organic Authority Engine or Authority Launchpad** — neither runs ads, so neither needs ad spend. Putting an ad-spend rider on an organic proposal quotes the client money they will never spend.
- What the math looks like — ads-managed tiers only: "Your total monthly investment at [Tier Name] is $[price] + ad spend". On Organic Authority Engine and Authority Launchpad the price is the whole number; state it and stop.

**Do not apologize for the price.** Do not justify it with ROI projections unless the user specifically asks for them. The proposal has already built the case — the diagnosis showed the problem, the approach showed the solution, the scope showed what they get. The price is just the number.

## Section 6: What Happens Next

One clear next step. Not three options. One.

Format:
1. A sentence that bridges from the pricing to the action: "If this looks like the right fit, here's what happens next."
2. The specific action: schedule a call, reply to this email, sign the attached agreement
3. What happens after they take that action: onboarding kickoff within [X] days, first strategy session, asset collection begins
4. A warm close from Brandon — 1–2 sentences that are personal, not corporate

**Example:**
"Reply to this email or text me at 402-819-8168 and we'll get your kickoff scheduled within the week. The first step is a 30-minute strategy session where we map out your first 90 days."

## Formatting rules for the .docx

- Page background: warm cream (#F0EDE8) or white (white is acceptable if cream doesn't render well in the docx engine)
- Section headers: Arial Black, 16–20pt, #1A1A1A or Accent Teal (#007A87)
- Body text: Arial, 11–12pt, #1A1A1A
- Table headers: Accent Teal (#007A87) background with white text
- Table body: alternating white / cream (#F8F5F0) rows
- Accent dividers: thin teal or gold lines between major sections
- Churlish Red (#C41E3A): used ONCE in the document — on the cover page branding or as a single accent element
- Page margins: 1 inch all sides (standard)
- No clip art, no stock photos, no decorative elements. Let the typography and whitespace do the work.
