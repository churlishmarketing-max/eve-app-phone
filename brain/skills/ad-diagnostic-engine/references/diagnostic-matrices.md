# Diagnostic Matrices

The two cross-metric matrices that drive all ad diagnoses. Read metric pairs, never single metrics. Each cell prescribes a specific action.

---

## Matrix 1 — Video Hook Rate × Hook-to-Lead Efficiency

**This is the primary diagnostic.** It tells you whether attention is converting into action — the difference between vanity metrics and revenue.

| Hook Rate ↓ \ Efficiency → | **Great (50%+)** | **Okay (40–49%)** | **Bad (<40%)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Great (20–30%+)** | 🚀 **Scale hard.** High volume + strong conversion. Don't touch. | People love the hook, but some drop off. Fix CTA + lead form friction. | Hook attracts unqualified people. Refine targeting + qualify audience better. |
| **Okay (10–19%)** | Test small hook tweaks to push Hook Rate to 20% without breaking efficiency. | ✅ **SWEET SPOT — don't break it.** Balanced and profitable. Don't over-optimize. | People don't engage and don't convert. Rework both hook and CTA. |
| **Bad (<10%)** | Lots of engagement, no action. Hook attracting wrong people. Tweak audience and messaging. | Not enough people engaging or converting. Audience doesn't resonate. Ad missing pain points. | 🔥 **Kill it.** Completely broken. Restart with new creative. |

### Sweet Spot Rule

**18% Hook Rate + 45% Efficiency is the proven Churlish formula.** Wins because:
- Gets enough attention (Hook Rate ~18%)
- Viewers convert at profitable rate (45%)
- Volume stays high enough to drive revenue

**Improvement strategy without breaking it:**
- Test small hook tweaks to push Hook Rate to 20% while maintaining efficiency
- Make sure offer + CTA match the pain point in the first 3 seconds
- Don't chase higher Hook Rate if it lowers efficiency — vanity over substance

### Hard Rule

**"Great" doesn't mean "Profitable."** A high Hook Rate with low Efficiency means eyeballs without revenue. A lower Hook Rate with high Efficiency can be more profitable than a viral-feeling ad. Always check the pair.

## Matrix 2 — Link CTR × CTR (All)

**This diagnoses whether engagement is converting into clicks.** Use it to decide whether the issue is the ad creative or the CTA.

| CTR All ↓ \ Link CTR → | **Great (1.0–1.5%+)** | **Okay (0.7–0.9%)** | **Bad (<0.7%)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Great (2.0–3.0%+)** | 🔥 **Ideal — scale.** High engagement + high intent clicks. | Engagement strong, link CTA weak. Test stronger CTA to push more to the link. | Engagement bait, no intent. Strip fluff and push direct CTA. |
| **Okay (1.0–1.9%)** | Direct-response winner. People are clicking instead of just engaging. Working well. | Balanced but underperforming. Test new hooks and add urgency. | Ad blending into the feed. New creative — colors, hook, motion. |
| **Bad (<1.0%)** | Hidden winner — highly targeted audience. May still be profitable if leads convert. | Weak ad — low CTR (All) + low Link CTR. Rework first 3 seconds and CTA. | 🔥 **Kill it.** Nobody is clicking. Start fresh. |

### Reading combinations

| Pattern | Diagnosis | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| High CTR (All), Low Link CTR | Engagement without intent | Stronger benefit-led CTA, more direct-response framing |
| Low CTR (All), Low Link CTR | Ad ignored | Improve first 3 seconds, high-contrast visuals, new copy |
| Low CTR (All), Okay Link CTR | Niche ad pulling qualified leads | Don't over-optimize — may still be profitable |
| Both Good | Working ad | Test version with more engagement elements (comments, social proof) |
| Both Good, Weak Leads | Targeting too broad | Refine audience to higher-intent users |

---

## Combined diagnostic flow

When the user shares ad data:

1. **Locate position on Matrix 1 first** (Hook Rate × Efficiency) — this is the primary action driver
2. **Locate position on Matrix 2** (Link CTR × CTR All) — this refines the prescription
3. **Cross-check single metrics** for any in Poor/Bad range (CPM, Frequency, CPL)
4. **Apply Frequency override** — if Frequency 3+, refresh creative regardless of other matrix positions
5. **Determine action:** Scale / Tweak / Refresh / Kill
6. **Flag the trade-off** if recommended fix risks breaking another metric

---

## Action types (every diagnosis ends in one)

| Action | When | What |
|---|---|---|
| **Scale** | Both matrices in Great cells, single metrics healthy | Increase budget, expand audience, duplicate to new placements |
| **Tweak** | One matrix Good/Okay, other in adjustment cell | Specific creative, CTA, or targeting change to test (always one variable at a time) |
| **Refresh** | Frequency 3+, ad fatigue signals, performance declining over time | Full creative rebuild — new hook, new visuals, new CTA |
| **Kill** | Both matrices in Bad/Bad cells | Stop the ad, start over with new concept |
