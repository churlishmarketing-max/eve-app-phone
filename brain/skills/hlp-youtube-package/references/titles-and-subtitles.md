# HLP Titles & Thumbnail Subtitles

## Titles

A High Level Pros title sells the **angle**, not the topic. For interview episodes, the click comes from a person + a hook: a contradiction, a transformation, or a hard number that shouldn't fit the person. Keep the recommended title under ~60 characters so it doesn't truncate.

### The three title angles (match to the episode's strongest pull)

**1. The contradiction** — an identity that shouldn't go with the outcome.
- "Cop by Day, 7-Figure Marketer by Night"
- "This Cop Built a Million-Dollar Agency at Night"

**2. The transformation gap** — origin → outcome, two ends snapped together.
- "Told He'd Be 'Nothing' — Now He's a Millionaire"
- "They Told Him He'd Be a 'Trash Man.' He Built a 7-Figure Agency."

**3. The tactical promise** — a concrete, anti-guru payoff (best when the episode's hook is a method or number).
- "The $25K Business Audit You Can Now Run for Free"
- "How an Ex-Cop Built a 7-Figure Agency"

### Title rules

- Quoted phrases that are real to the episode ("dirt," "trash man," "nothing") hit harder than paraphrase — they signal *someone actually said this*.
- Numbers earn the click when they're true and surprising ($60K/mo, 7 figures, $25K).
- Deliver one recommended + 3–4 alternates, each labeled with its angle.
- **A/B testing:** when the user is testing two thumbnails/titles, keep the title variants in **parallel structure** so the test isolates the *angle*, not the sentence shape. Example: if one is `[origin] TO [outcome]`, the other should be too — "DIRT TO MILLIONS" vs "COP TO MILLIONAIRE" is a clean experiment; pitting a quote-title against a how-to title is not.

## Thumbnail subtitles (on-image text)

The text on the thumbnail is **not** the title. It's 2–4 words whose power comes from the **gap** between them, read in under a second. The image carries the rest — never make the text re-explain the picture.

### Formulas

**The two-word gap** (strongest for a transformation composite):
- `DIRT TO MILLIONS`
- `COP TO MILLIONAIRE`
- `BADGE TO 7 FIGURES`

**Let the image finish the sentence** (pairs with a payoff visual like cash/skyline):
- `"YOU'RE NOTHING."`
- `HE WAS A COP.`

**The quoted wound** (real line from the episode, one word in red):
- `THEY CALLED HIM DIRT` (red: DIRT)
- `HE PROVED THEM WRONG` (red: WRONG)

### Subtitle rules

- 2–4 words. If it needs more, it's a title, not a subtitle.
- The red-accent treatment = one word in red, the rest white/cream. Pick the word that carries the punch (the wound or the payoff).
- Keep it to ≤2 lines and big — 80%+ of clicks happen at phone size, where a short bold line beats a full-width sentence fighting the face.
- For A/B tests, keep both subtitles in the same structure (e.g. both `[origin] TO [outcome]`) so the variable under test is the *origin story*, not the format.
- Deliver one recommended + 2–3 alternates; note the red word.
