# Strategy Document Templates

## Strategic gameplan template

This is the flagship strategy document. It tells the client "here's exactly what we're building and why."

### Section structure:

**1. Cover page**
- Client business name (large)
- "2026 Strategic Gameplan" (or appropriate year/quarter)
- "Prepared by Churlish Media"
- Date

**2. Executive summary**
One paragraph. The "if you read nothing else, read this" distillation. State the revenue target, the strategic approach in one sentence, and the primary lever being pulled. Example: "This gameplan maps GE Outdoors to a $507K revenue year by turning your projects into a content series, running targeted CTV and Meta advertising to Omaha-area homeowners, and building an asset library that compounds over time."

**3. Situation analysis**
Where the client is right now. Include:
- Current revenue (actual or estimated)
- Current marketing activities and their effectiveness
- Competitive landscape (how many competitors, what they're doing)
- Gaps and opportunities identified
- If other vendors are in the picture, name them and describe their role without disparaging them

Tone: diagnostic, factual, respectful. This section builds credibility by showing you've done your homework.

**4. Revenue model**
Break the target down into achievable components:
- Revenue by service type or product line (with estimated ticket sizes)
- Seasonal patterns (which months are strong/weak)
- Required lead volume to hit targets (work backward from close rate)
- Average deal size assumptions (flag as estimates when using market data)

Present as a table with monthly or quarterly columns. Always include the math — show how the numbers add up to the target.

**5. Content strategy**
What content gets produced and why:
- The "big idea" or content concept (e.g., "Turn projects into a show" for GE Outdoors)
- Content types and their purpose (authority videos, short-form clips, educational content, ad creative)
- Platform priority and why
- The "one shoot, multiple channels" leverage framework
- Repurposing cascade — how one shoot becomes N deliverables

**6. Monthly deliverables**
A table showing exactly what Churlish delivers each month:
- Video shoots (quantity)
- Short-form pieces (quantity)
- Photos (quantity)
- Ad creatives (quantity)
- Blog posts or written content (if applicable)
- Strategy calls (frequency)
- Any additional deliverables specific to this engagement

**7. Marketing calendar**
Seasonal or quarterly view of campaigns, content themes, and promotional periods. Map content intensity to the client's business cycles (e.g., spring/summer heavy for outdoor services).

**8. Budget framework**
Break down the total marketing investment:
- Churlish monthly retainer
- Recommended ad spend by platform
- Any additional costs (CTV, radio, tools/software)
- Total monthly investment
- Annual total

Position the budget as an investment, not an expense. Where possible, show the relationship between spend and expected outcome.

**9. Responsibility matrix**
A table with three columns: Task, Churlish Media, Client (and optionally a third column for other vendors). Clear check marks or ownership labels for every major activity. This eliminates confusion about who does what — especially important when other vendors are in the picture.

**10. Timeline**
Month-by-month or phase-by-phase view of what happens when:
- Month 1: Onboarding, asset collection, first shoot, system setup
- Month 2–3: Content machine running, ads launching, initial data
- Month 4–6: Optimization, scaling what works, expanding content library
- Month 7–12: Compounding phase — the asset library is deep, the funnel is proven, the system runs

**11. Appendix (optional)**
Supporting materials — competitor screenshots, market data sources, detailed technical specs, or reference documents.

## KPI tracker template

### Format: .xlsx with monthly tabs or a single master tab

**Columns:**
- Metric name
- Target (monthly)
- Actual (filled in during review)
- Variance (% over/under)
- Notes

**Metric categories:**

*Social media:*
- Followers (by platform)
- Post reach (average)
- Engagement rate
- Video views
- Profile visits
- Link clicks

*Paid advertising:*
- Ad spend (by platform)
- Impressions
- Click-through rate
- Cost per click
- Leads generated
- Cost per lead
- ROAS (if e-commerce)

*CTV / broadcast (if applicable):*
- Impressions served
- Frequency
- Estimated reach
- Attribution method and results

*Website / SEO:*
- Website sessions
- Unique visitors
- Top landing pages
- Organic search impressions
- Google Business Profile views and actions

*Revenue attribution:*
- Total leads (by source)
- Appointments booked
- Close rate
- Revenue booked (by source)
- Customer acquisition cost
- Lifetime value (if data available)

**Monthly review questions (include 8–10 per tracker):**
1. Which channel produced the most leads this month?
2. Which ad creative performed best? Why?
3. What content got the most organic engagement?
4. Are we on pace for the quarterly revenue target?
5. What's the cost per lead trend — improving or declining?
6. Which content should we produce more of based on performance?
7. What didn't work this month and what are we adjusting?
8. Are there any new opportunities we should test next month?
9. Is the client showing up for their part (shoots, approvals, follow-up)?
10. What does the client need to hear in the next strategy call?

## Content strategy / playbook template

### Section structure:

**1. Brand voice snapshot** — Quick reference for the client's tone and style (pulled from avatar bible if available)

**2. Content pillars** — 3–5 pillar definitions with examples of each

**3. Platform strategy** — Which platforms, posting frequency, and why each platform matters for this client

**4. Video strategy** — Long-form concepts, short-form clip approach, authority video framework

**5. Repurposing framework** — How each content type cascades into multiple assets

**6. Funnel mix** — TOF/MOF/BOF ratios and what each stage looks like for this client

**7. Batch production plan** — Shoot schedule, editing workflow, and publishing cadence

**8. First 90 days** — Specific content plan for the first 3 months (more detailed than the ongoing calendar)

## Revenue model template

### Format: .xlsx with the following tabs

**Tab 1: Revenue by service/product**
- Service/product name
- Estimated average ticket size
- Estimated monthly volume
- Monthly revenue
- Annual revenue
- Source of estimate (market data, client input, Churlish benchmark)

**Tab 2: Seasonal adjustment**
- Monthly multipliers (e.g., 1.3x for peak season, 0.7x for slow season)
- Adjusted monthly revenue targets

**Tab 3: Lead math**
- Revenue target ÷ average ticket = required jobs/sales
- Required jobs ÷ close rate = required leads
- Required leads ÷ conversion rate = required traffic/impressions
- This backward math shows the client exactly what the marketing needs to produce

**Tab 4: Investment vs. return**
- Monthly marketing investment (all sources)
- Monthly revenue target
- Marketing as % of revenue
- Break-even point
- Projected ROI at 6/12 months
