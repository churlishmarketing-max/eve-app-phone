# KPI Frameworks

## Core principle

Every KPI must connect to revenue. Vanity metrics (followers, likes, impressions) are only meaningful when they ladder up to leads, appointments, and closed deals. Every tracker Churlish builds shows the chain from content → engagement → leads → revenue.

## The attribution chain

For every client, map this chain and build KPIs at each stage:

```
Content published → Reach/Impressions → Engagement → Click/Visit → Lead captured → Appointment booked → Deal closed → Revenue
```

The tracker should measure at least 3 points in this chain. Which 3 depends on the client's business model and what data is available.

## KPI benchmarks by industry

Use these as starting assumptions when the client doesn't have historical data. Flag all benchmarks as estimates and refine with actual data after 60–90 days.

### Home services (roofing, HVAC, plumbing, etc.)
- Average ticket: $3,000–$15,000 (varies by service)
- Close rate from lead: 20–35%
- Cost per lead (Meta ads): $15–$50
- Monthly lead target for $500K annual: 8–15 jobs/month depending on ticket size
- Content engagement rate benchmark: 2–4%

### Fitness / coaching / wellness
- Average ticket: $100–$300/month (memberships) or $1,000–$5,000 (programs)
- Close rate from lead: 15–30%
- Cost per lead (Meta ads): $5–$25
- Content engagement rate benchmark: 3–6% (higher than most verticals)

### Professional services (law, accounting, financial)
- Average ticket: $2,000–$25,000+ per engagement
- Close rate from lead: 10–25% (higher trust barrier)
- Cost per lead (Meta ads): $25–$75
- Content engagement rate benchmark: 1–3%

### E-commerce / DTC
- Average order value: varies widely
- ROAS benchmark: 3–5x for established brands, 2–3x for new
- Cost per acquisition: varies by product price
- Email conversion rate: 2–5%
- Cart abandonment rate: 65–75% (industry standard)

### Woodworking / artisan / maker
- Average ticket: $200–$5,000 per piece
- Margins: 20–35% for small operations
- Cost per lead: $10–$30 (niche targeting is cheaper)
- The key metric here is often margin improvement (reducing supply costs) rather than lead volume

## Revenue modeling approaches

### Approach 1: Top-down (target → required activity)
Start with the annual revenue target. Work backward through close rate, lead volume, and required marketing activity to determine what the content and ad system needs to produce.

Best for: clients with a clear revenue goal and enough historical data to estimate close rates.

### Approach 2: Bottom-up (current capacity → projected revenue)
Start with what the client can actually deliver (jobs per month, clients served, products shipped). Calculate the revenue ceiling at current capacity, then identify where marketing can push utilization toward that ceiling.

Best for: capacity-constrained businesses (trades, service providers) where the bottleneck is leads, not delivery.

### Approach 3: Benchmark-based (industry average → adjusted projection)
Use industry data to model what a business of their size and type should be producing, then identify the gap between their actual performance and the benchmark.

Best for: new engagements where the client has limited data, or when presenting a case for why marketing investment is warranted.

## Tracker cadence

- **Weekly:** Ad spend, lead count, content published (quick pulse check)
- **Monthly:** Full KPI review across all categories, strategy call with client
- **Quarterly:** Revenue attribution analysis, strategy adjustment, budget reallocation
- **Annual:** Full year review, next year planning, case study production

## The monthly review framework

Every monthly strategy call should cover these 5 areas:

1. **What worked** — Top-performing content, best ad creative, highest-converting channel
2. **What didn't** — Underperformers, wasted spend, missed targets
3. **What we're adjusting** — Specific changes for next month based on data
4. **What the client needs to do** — Any actions on their side (show up for shoots, approve content, follow up with leads)
5. **The pipeline** — Where revenue stands vs. target, what's in the pipeline, what needs to close
