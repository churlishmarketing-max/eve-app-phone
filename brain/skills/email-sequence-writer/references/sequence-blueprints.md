# Sequence Blueprints

Proven email sequence structures for common Churlish automation scenarios. Each blueprint includes the number of emails, timing, the objective of each email, and the psychological principle it applies.

## Sequence A: Never booked (Opt-in → no call scheduled)

**Trigger:** Someone opts in (lead magnet, contact form, landing page) but doesn't schedule a discovery call.
**Goal:** Get them on the phone.
**Emails:** 5
**Duration:** 21 days
**Exit:** If they book a call at any point, remove from sequence.

| Email | Day | Objective | Principle |
|-------|-----|-----------|-----------|
| A-1 | Day 0 | Welcome + immediate value. Introduce who you are, deliver the lead magnet (if applicable), and plant the seed for the call. Include one proof point. | First impression — warm, human, zero pressure |
| A-2 | Day 3 | Handle the "will this work for me?" objection. Address the most common reason people in this avatar's situation hesitate. Use a question format. | NEPQ — pull, don't push |
| A-3 | Day 7 | Deep case study. One client story with before/after/timeline. Specific numbers. Let the story do the selling. | Proof stacking — specific > general |
| A-4 | Day 14 | Cost of inaction. Reframe inaction as a choice with real consequences. Use actual math (what they're losing per month by not fixing the problem). | Loss aversion — the gap between current state and potential |
| A-5 | Day 21 | Graceful exit. Acknowledge they may not be ready. Remove pressure. Leave the door open. Use the Voss "no-oriented question" — "Have you given up on this?" | The Voss close — a "no" that actually means engagement |

## Sequence B: No-show (Call scheduled → missed)

**Trigger:** Discovery call was scheduled but the prospect didn't show.
**Goal:** Rebook the call.
**Emails:** 3
**Duration:** 7 days
**Exit:** If they rebook, remove from sequence. If no response after Email B-3, move to Sequence A starting at Email A-3 (skip the intro — they're already warm).

| Email | Day | Objective | Principle |
|-------|-----|-----------|-----------|
| B-1 | Day 0 (within 1 hour) | Warm, zero-guilt acknowledgment. "Life happens." Offer easy rebook. | Remove shame — shame kills re-engagement |
| B-2 | Day 2 | Rebuild the value of the call itself. Briefly describe what they would have gotten from the conversation. Include a mini-result from a past call. | Remind them what they're missing — value, not pressure |
| B-3 | Day 7 | The Voss loop-closer. "It seems like now might not be the right time. Should I close your file?" This question almost always generates a response because people don't like being written off. | No-oriented question — triggers re-engagement |

## Sequence C: Open proposal (Proposal sent → no response)

**Trigger:** Proposal was delivered but no response after 48 hours.
**Goal:** Get a decision (yes or no — either is better than silence).
**Emails:** 3
**Duration:** 14 days
**Exit:** If they respond (accept, reject, or ask questions), remove from sequence.

| Email | Day | Objective | Principle |
|-------|-----|-----------|-----------|
| C-1a (opened) | Day 2 | Acknowledge they've looked at it. Ask if anything needs clarification. Short, personal, low-pressure. | Mirror their behavior — "I noticed you took a look" |
| C-1b (not opened) | Day 2 | Check if they received it. Resend with a different subject line. Sometimes proposals get buried. | Benefit of the doubt — assume logistics, not rejection |
| C-2 | Day 7 | Cost-of-inaction email. Requires manual customization — reference specific numbers from their discovery call. "Every month without [the system] is another month of [specific consequence]." | Personalized loss aversion — their numbers, not generic |
| C-3 | Day 14 | The Voss close. "Have you decided to go a different direction? Either way, I'd love to know so I can plan accordingly." Clean, respectful, and almost impossible to ignore. | No-oriented question — forces a response |

**Note on C-2:** Flag this email in the automation as requiring a manual step. The sending person needs to insert 1–2 specific numbers or details from the discovery call. A generic "you're losing money" email is far less effective than "At your current lead volume, you're leaving roughly $X on the table every month."

## Sequence D: General nurture (Monthly, ongoing)

**Trigger:** Anyone in the database who isn't in an active sequence or an active client.
**Goal:** Stay top of mind. Provide value. Be the first call when they're ready.
**Emails:** 3 rotating topics (sent monthly, rotating each month)
**Duration:** Ongoing until they convert or unsubscribe.

| Email | Topic | Objective | Principle |
|-------|-------|-----------|-----------|
| D-1 | The Consideration Bridge | Insight-driven email about a concept relevant to the avatar's situation. Not a pitch — a useful perspective. End with a soft CTA ("If this resonated, reply and tell me what you're working on"). | Value-first — earn the right to stay in their inbox |
| D-2 | Scaling vs. plateauing | Observation about what separates businesses that grow from those that stall. Reference a real example. Connect it to the services without pitching directly. | Pattern recognition — the reader should see themselves |
| D-3 | Why [channel] isn't converting | Diagnostic email about why their content/ads/website isn't producing results. Specific, tactical, actionable even without hiring anyone. End with "If you want help diagnosing this for your business, hit reply." | Give away the playbook — the ones who can execute it alone weren't your clients anyway. The ones who can't will hire you. |

## Sequence E: Post-purchase / new client welcome

**Trigger:** Client signs the agreement and pays the first invoice.
**Goal:** Set expectations, build excitement, and start the relationship strong.
**Emails:** 4
**Duration:** 14 days

| Email | Day | Objective | Principle |
|-------|-----|-----------|-----------|
| E-1 | Day 0 | Immediate confirmation and welcome. What happens next (onboarding call within X days). What to prepare (asset list, access credentials, brand materials). | Reduce buyer's remorse — speed and clarity |
| E-2 | Day 1 | The "what to expect" email. Month 1 looks like X. Month 2 looks like Y. By month 3, here's where we should be. Set realistic expectations upfront. | Expectation setting — prevents disappointment and builds trust |
| E-3 | Day 7 | Check-in after kickoff call. Recap what was discussed. Confirm the plan. Ask if anything was missed. | Active listening confirmation — "we heard you" |
| E-4 | Day 14 | First milestone update. Even if it's small ("your first batch of content is in editing" or "your ads are in review"), show progress. | Momentum — early wins build confidence in the relationship |

## Sequence F: Offboarding / renewal (Client approaching end of commitment)

**Trigger:** 60 days before commitment end date.
**Goal:** Renew, upgrade, or transition to a maintenance arrangement.
**Emails:** 3
**Duration:** 60 days

| Email | Day | Objective | Principle |
|-------|-----|-----------|-----------|
| F-1 | Day 0 (60 days out) | Soft heads-up. "Your engagement wraps up in 60 days. Let's start talking about what comes next." No pressure — just opening the conversation. | Early framing — gives time to think instead of forcing a snap decision |
| F-2 | Day 30 (30 days out) | Results recap + renewal options. Summarize what was built, what the numbers look like, and present renewal/upgrade paths. Use a comparison format. | Proof of value — remind them what they'd lose |
| F-3 | Day 45 (15 days out) | Decision email. "I want to make sure we have a plan either way. Are we continuing, adjusting, or wrapping up? Happy with any answer — just want to plan accordingly." | The Voss respectful close — any answer is acceptable |

## Building custom sequences

When the user asks for a sequence type not listed here, follow this framework:

1. **Define the trigger** — What event starts the sequence?
2. **Define the goal** — What action do we want the reader to take?
3. **Map the emotional arc** — Where is the reader mentally at each stage? (Curious → Considering → Hesitant → Ready, or Engaged → Distracted → Re-engaged)
4. **Choose the right length** — Shorter for time-sensitive sequences (no-show, open proposal), longer for nurture
5. **Apply the Voss/NEPQ principles** — Mirror, label, question. Never push.
6. **End with grace** — Every sequence needs an exit ramp that respects the reader's decision
