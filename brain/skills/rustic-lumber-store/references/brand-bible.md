# Rustic Lumber Store / Woodaddy — Brand Bible

## Business Overview

**Business Name:** Rustic Lumber Store / Woodaddy (product brand)
**Location:** Omaha, Nebraska
**Founder:** Zach Witkin
**Business Model:** Vertically integrated woodworking operation — sells the raw material, builds the finished product, AND manufactures the finish
**Team Size:** ~3-5 people
**Revenue Target:** $1,000,000/year across three engines
**Strategist:** Brandon King — Churlish Media (CMO role)

### The "Costco Hot Dog" Model
RLS has multiple revenue streams (wood sales, custom furniture, timber framing) that keep the business afloat. The finish doesn't have to carry the whole business. This structural advantage allows lower oil prices. Most standalone finish brands MUST charge premium prices to survive — RLS doesn't. Zach uses the Costco analogy: "Costco sells a hot dog for $1.50 because they make money from everything else in the store. If they opened a standalone hot dog shop, they'd be out of business."

### Why Woodaddy Was Created
- 4 years ago, couldn't find a finish with the right quality + price + customer service
- Big brands were too big to give personal service, wanted $5K-$10K minimum wholesale orders
- Created a two-part oil system (oil + hardener) that works exactly like Rubio Monocoat at 2/3 the cost
- Originally called "Rustic Lumber Store Finish," rebranded to Woodaddy 2 years ago for standalone identity

## Target Demographics

### Oil Demographics (Designers REMOVED — they're in the furniture demographic)

**Persona 1: The Hobbyist Woodworker (55% of oil volume)**
- Age 30-65, predominantly male, HHI $60K-$150K
- Weekend garage shop builder. 3-5 hours woodworking YouTube/week.
- Follows: Blacktail Studio, Stumpy Nubs, Jonathan Katz-Moses, Bourbon Moth
- Buying trigger: Creator endorsement in a build video
- Average order: $60-$75
- Channels: YouTube, Facebook Groups, Instagram, Google Search

**Persona 2: The Professional Builder (25% of oil volume, highest revenue %)**
- Age 28-55, $60K-$500K/year custom furniture business
- Buys in bulk (3000ml). Extremely loyal once they find a product that works.
- Buying trigger: Peer recommendation
- Average order: $160-$220
- Channels: YouTube, word-of-mouth, Instagram, trade shows

**Persona 3: The DIY Home Renovator (20% of oil volume)**
- Age 28-50, M/F, HHI $80K-$200K
- Specific project (refinish dining table, restore dresser, butcher block)
- Buying trigger: Tutorial content featuring the product
- Average order: $10-$70
- Channels: Google Search, Pinterest, YouTube How-To, Instagram

### Furniture Demographics
- **Homeowners/Clients:** Age 30-65, HHI $100K+, Omaha metro area. AOV $4,000-$12,000.
- **Interior Designers / Commercial:** Specifying for client projects. Trade accounts. AOV $5,000-$25,000. 10-20 projects/year.

### Lumber Demographics
- Hobbyist woodworkers + professional builders (same as oil, but in-shop)
- 85% in-shop purchases, 15% website (high-value slabs $500+ only)

## Competitive Landscape

| Brand | Price/mL | Tier | RLS Positioning Against |
|-------|----------|------|----------------------|
| Rubio Monocoat | $0.12-$0.24 | Ultra-Premium | RESPECT them. "Honest about their product." 3-4× more expensive for same result. |
| Odie's Oil | $0.22-$0.24 | Ultra-Premium | DO NOT respect the marketing. Scare tactics. "Tinfoil hat." Porsche in the shop. Product itself is "not bad." |
| Osmo | $0.07-$0.08 | Premium | German import, weak DTC. Higher per-mL than RLS. |
| **Rustic Lumber Store** | **$0.053-$0.067** | **Accessible Premium** | **The only brand with maker credibility + accessible pricing** |
| Tried & True | $0.045-$0.068 | Premium | Heritage recipes, dealer-focused. |
| Walrus Oil | $0.037-$0.055 | Mid-Range | Respect. Founder story. But below RLS in price band. |
| Pure Tung/RMP | $0.020-$0.035 | Budget | Commodity. No story. |
| Howard Products | $0.020-$0.025 | Budget | Mass market. No story. |

**Key Competitive Insight:** RLS is the ONLY brand occupying accessible premium + maker story + Midwest identity. No competitor can claim all three. But lead with performance and maker credibility — NOT geography.

**The Unique RLS Advantage:** One unified brand offers oil, poly acrylic, AND epoxy. Every other brand is one or the other: oil brands (Rubio, Odie's, Osmo) OR poly brands (Renner, Gemini). RLS gives you every finish type under one roof. Zach is explicit about this: "I'm not here to tell you our oil is the only thing you ever need. Different projects need different finishes."

## Strategy Decisions Log (Non-Negotiable)

| Decision | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|

## Brand Voice Guide

### Tone
- Honest, direct, no-BS, blue-collar, maker-first
- Confident but never arrogant — "this is a great product" not "this is the best product"
- Self-deprecating about the operation size: "three dudes in a shop," "transit van," "tarp for a doorway"
- Transparent about money: will publicly state profit per can, pricing math, business model
- Anti-corporate: hates scare tactics, hates inflated claims, hates paying for brand names

### Words/Phrases Zach USES
- "Honest woodworker," "common man," "the small guys"
- "Fraction of the cost," "pretty much the same"
- "Not here to be a salesperson"
- "Big oil" (sarcastically about Odie's calling competitors this)
- "Transit van" (his vehicle vs. competitor's Porsche)
- "We package every order right in our shop"
- "Our bread and butter are the small guys"

### Words/Phrases to NEVER Use
- "Best in the world," "outperforms everything" (this is what Odie's does and Zach hates it)
- "Revolutionary," "game-changing," "disrupting" (corporate language)
- "Premium luxury" (the brand is premium in QUALITY but accessible in PRICE)
- Any fear-based health claims about competitor products
- "You NEED this" — Zach explicitly says every product has its place
