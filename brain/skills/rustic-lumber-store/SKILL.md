## Quick Reference — The Non-Negotiables

These rules apply to EVERY piece of content or strategy work for RLS:

### Brand Identity
- **Business:** Rustic Lumber Store — vertically integrated woodworking operation in Omaha, NE
- **Product Brand:** Woodaddy (furniture oil, 2K poly acrylic, epoxy)
- **Founder:** Zach Witkin — furniture builder, not a finish salesman
- **Team:** ~3-5 people ("three dudes in a shop")
- **Positioning Lead:** Performance + Maker credibility FIRST, Midwest identity as brand texture underneath (NEVER lead with geography)

### The Three Revenue Engines
1. **Custom Furniture** ($400K-$450K target) — tables, desks, shelving, mantels, commercial
2. **Raw Lumber & Slabs** ($250K-$300K target) — hardwood slabs, live edge, dimensional, specialty
3. **Woodaddy Finishes** ($250K-$300K target) — furniture oil, 2K poly acrylic, epoxy

### Channel Rules (Hard Constraints)
- Sales come from **website + in-shop ONLY**
- **NO Amazon** — removed deliberately for margin control and customer ownership
- **NO retail partners / storefronts** — no wholesale distribution to other stores
- Designers are in the **furniture demographic**, NOT the oil demographic

### Brand Voice Rules
- Honest, direct, no-BS. Zach speaks like a woodworker, not a marketer.
- NEVER claim the oil is "the best in the world" or "outperforms everything" — that's what competitors do and Zach explicitly hates it
- ALWAYS acknowledge other products have their place: "Every project has a purpose for every different product"
- Lead with VALUE, not superiority: "Same result, fraction of the cost"
- The Costco analogy is a core brand narrative: low overhead from multiple revenue streams allows lower oil prices
- Radical transparency is the brand's superpower: "$30 profit per can and I'll say it out loud"

### Key Verbatim Quotes (Use in Content)
- "I'm not here to be a salesperson. I'm here to just be an honest woodworker offering a good product for the common man."
- "We make like 30 bucks a can and that doesn't include us paying shipping."
- "I got three dudes working here. Big oil."
- "I can park my transit van behind me."
- "I ain't going to sit here and try to lie to anyone that this is going to outperform anything in the world that chemists have been working on for over a hundred years."
- "Everyone that's used it has said there's no tangible difference in quality."
- "900ml cans for $60, and Rubio is 350ml for like 70 to $80."

### Competitive Positioning
- **vs. Rubio Monocoat:** Respect them. "Honest about their product." Price is high, you're paying for the brand. RLS is 3-4× cheaper per mL with comparable results.
- **vs. Odie's Oil:** Do NOT respect the marketing. Scare tactics, "tinfoil hat," claims product is best in the world. Porsche parked behind him. BUT acknowledge the product itself is "not bad" for cutting boards.
- **vs. Walrus Oil:** Acknowledge as quality. Differentiate on maker heritage and price band position.
- **vs. Osmo:** German import, weak DTC, higher price per mL.

- Deadly serious delivery about woodworking products (humor = contrast)
- Can transition to real Zach mid-script for substance moments

- Built from real Zach quotes, just amplified
