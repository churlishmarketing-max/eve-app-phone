### Current situation

[2–3 paragraphs describing what this person's professional life looks like day-to-day. What are they doing for marketing right now? How are they getting customers? What's working and what's not? What does a typical week look like?]

**Guidance:** Write this in third person present tense, as if observing their life. "He posts on Facebook when he remembers, maybe twice a week. The posts get 3–5 likes from friends and family. He tried boosting a post once and got 200 clicks that turned into zero calls. His best marketing channel is word of mouth and yard signs."

### Pain points

List 5–8 specific pain points, stated in the avatar's own language:

1. [Pain point — in their words, not yours]
2. [Pain point]
3. [Pain point]
4. [Pain point]
5. [Pain point]

**Guidance:** The most powerful pain points are the ones the avatar says out loud — to their spouse, to their buddy, in a Facebook group. NOT what they'd write in a formal survey. "I'm tired of competing with guys who do shit work but show up first on Google" is a pain point. "Difficulty with digital discoverability" is a marketing abstraction.

**Common pain point categories to explore:**
- Visibility / discoverability (nobody knows they exist)
- Lead generation (they don't know where the next client is coming from)
- Price competition (they're being undercut by inferior competitors)
- Time scarcity (they're too busy doing the work to market the work)
- Inconsistency (feast or famine cycle)
- Credibility (they're good but can't prove it at scale)
- Wasted spend (they've tried things that didn't work)
- Comparison (they see competitors growing and don't understand why)

### Goals / aspirations

List 3–5 goals, ranging from immediate to aspirational:

1. [Immediate goal — e.g., "Get 10 consistent leads per month"]
2. [Short-term goal — e.g., "Hit $500K in annual revenue"]
3. [Medium-term goal — e.g., "Hire a full-time office manager so I can stay in the field"]
4. [Aspirational goal — e.g., "Be the name everyone in the metro thinks of for [service]"]
5. [Personal goal — e.g., "Take a real vacation without worrying the phone won't ring when I get back"]

**Guidance:** Goals should escalate from tactical to emotional. The tactical goals (more leads, more revenue) are what they'll say on a discovery call. The emotional goals (freedom, recognition, security) are why they actually buy. Both matter.

### Objections

List 4–6 objections they'll raise before buying:

1. **"[Objection in their exact words]"**
   *What they actually mean:* [The real concern underneath]
   *How to handle:* [Brief response strategy]

2. **"[Objection]"**
   *What they actually mean:* [...]
   *How to handle:* [...]

**Guidance:** The best objections come directly from discovery calls. The second-best come from understanding the avatar's decision-making psychology. Common objection patterns:
- "I can't afford it" → usually means "I'm not sure it'll work"
- "I need to think about it" → usually means "I don't have enough information to decide"
- "I have a guy for that" → usually means "I'm not unhappy enough to change yet"
- "My business is different" → usually means "I'm afraid this is a generic solution"
- "Can you guarantee results?" → usually means "I've been burned before"

### Trigger events

What makes them ready to act NOW? List 3–5 trigger events:

1. [e.g., "Slow season hits and they realize they have no pipeline"]
2. [e.g., "A competitor launches a YouTube channel and starts getting their clients"]
3. [e.g., "They get a 1-star review that tanks their Google ranking"]
4. [e.g., "They calculate how much they spent on a bad agency last year"]
5. [e.g., "Someone refers a job to the competitor because 'I couldn't find you online'"]

**Guidance:** Trigger events are the moments that turn passive problem-awareness into active solution-seeking. These events should inform ad timing (seasonal triggers), hook writing (reference the trigger in the call-out), and content themes (create content about the trigger before it happens so you're already in their feed when it does).

### Language patterns

Capture 5–10 phrases that this avatar actually uses when describing their situation. These can be direct quotes from discovery calls, representative phrasing from industry forums, or language patterns you've observed.

- "[Phrase they'd say about their problem]"
- "[Phrase they'd say about their goal]"
- "[Phrase they'd say about marketing/agencies]"
- "[Phrase they'd say about competitors]"
- "[Phrase they'd say about money]"

**Guidance:** This is the single most important section for writing copy that converts. When an ad uses the exact language the reader uses in their own head, it creates the "they're talking to me" feeling. Pull from: discovery call transcripts, Facebook group posts, Google reviews of competitors, Reddit threads, and industry forums.
