 // 01 · THE RULE OF THE THREAD · BEFORE ANYTHING SENDS
 An invitation asks for a chair. Nothing else.

 RULE 01
 Zero service pitches, ever
 No Churlish offer, link, case study or "by the way" inside an invitation thread. One is a failure, and Watchtower checks for it daily. Owners talk. "That show is a sales funnel" costs five bookings.

 RULE 02
 Line one is about them
 The specific thing they did: the second location, the truck count, the award, the year they doubled. Never "I came across your business." If Kid Flash cannot find the specific thing, the name is not ready to send.

 RULE 03
 Plain words, five answers
 How long. Where. What you will ask. Who watches. What they walk away with. No house vocabulary reaches a guest: no "Authority Engine," no "debrief," no "Breakthrough Arc."

 ▲ The honest answer to "who is even watching?"
 The channel is under 1,000 subscribers. Do not dress that up and do not apologize for it. The invitation sells three true things: the room (a cinema-grade set, not a Zoom call), the short list (24 chairs this season, picked by hand, Omaha owners only), and what they leave with (the full episode, a trailer and two clips, free). Never name a guest who has not published yet. Never inflate a number.

 // 05 · THE PRE-INTERVIEW
 Fifteen minutes. Not recorded.

 Brandon runs it. It finds the episode, clears the numbers and sets the one rule. After it, Brandon confirms the Guest Score and picks the modules: one stage module, one specialty module, three or four industry questions.
 PI The pre-interview call 15 MIN · BRANDON
 GOAL
Find the stuck number, the three mistakes and the specialty.
 DONE WHEN
Score confirmed, numbers cleared, handoff to the Guest Coordinator sent.

 OPEN > "This is not the interview. It is fifteen minutes so the interview is good. Nothing here is recorded."
 NUMBER > "What is the number the business sat at the longest? How long were you there? Where is it now?" Vague answers get a mirror: "'A couple years'?"
 MISTAKES > "Looking back, what were the three things keeping you at that number?" Write them down in their words. These become segment two.
 TEACH > "What is the one thing you do better than almost anyone in your trade? Could you teach it in fifteen minutes so somebody could use it Monday?"
 CLEAR > "Which numbers are you fine saying on camera? Revenue, margin, what you paid yourself? Anything off limits?" Nothing goes on air that was not cleared here.
 RULE > "One rule on the show: nobody pitches. When I ask where people can find you, you get one answer." If they are a client or a sponsor: "and we say so on air."
 NEXT > "Who is an owner you respect who should sit in this chair?" Then: " [COORDINATOR] will send dates, the address and a one-page release."

 // 07 · RECORDING DAY
 Ninety minutes, on one sheet

 Clock What happens Owner
 T−30 Set check: cameras, audio, slate, lights. Release on file, or no record. Questions printed: spine + 1 stage module + 1 specialty module + 3–4 industry questions + the Ledger five. Brandon · Coordinator
 0:00 Arrival. Coffee. Walk the set. Small talk that is not about marketing. Brandon
 0:10 Mic up. Shoot the two-person thumbnail stills now, while everyone is fresh. Three expressions, both in frame. Brandon
 0:15 Repeat the two rules: cleared numbers only, nobody pitches. Brandon
 0:20 Record, 35–45 minutes. The stuck number lands inside the first 30 seconds of segment one. Brandon
 1:05 Pick-ups. Clean takes of the five Ledger answers. "Who should sit here next?" on camera. Brandon
 1:15 Thank them. Give the publish date. Walk them out. Brandon
 Same day Footage backed up twice. Ledger answers logged. Note to Lois Lane: the hardest line (cold open) and five clip candidates with rough timecodes. Brandon · Coordinator
 ★ Never on recording day
 Any offer, price, package or "we should talk about your marketing."
 "So what are you doing for marketing right now?" That question belongs to the call after the episode publishes.
 A leave-behind, a rate card, a case study on the table.
 Anything you would not want the guest to repeat to the next owner they have lunch with.
